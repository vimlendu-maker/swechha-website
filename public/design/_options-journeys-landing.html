Journeys landing page — three options

/work/journeys — the vertical's overview: what a journey is, the idea behind it, and the four ways in. Same language as the homepage throughout. What differs is whether the four journeys are comparable at a glance or experienced one at a time.

Option A

The idea, then four unequal doors

A short editorial opening carries the idea, then the four journeys sit as tiles of different sizes — Yamuna Yatra double-width because it is the flagship, the other three at equal weight. Each tile shows its route or region and one line. All four are visible in one screen, so a visitor can compare and choose.

Recommended. Fastest to choose from, and §3.1's ban is on a grid of equal cards — unequal tiles are explicitly what it asks for instead. Also the most forgiving of uneven photography: the flagship needs one strong frame, the rest can be merely good.

Option B

One idea full-screen, then four bands

The page opens on the idea alone — a single line on black, near-empty, the pause treatment the About section uses. Then each journey is a full-bleed horizontal band with its own photograph and a copy plate, stacked. The four detail pages then feel like continuations of these bands.

Most atmospheric, and the most consistent with the journey detail pages. The cost is comparison: the four are never on screen together, so choosing means scrolling through all of them — and it needs four strong photographs, not one.

Option C

An index, routes forward

The idea in two sentences, then the four as an index: thumbnail, route set in the data voice, duration, one line, and a link. Reads like a contents page for the vertical — scannable, dense, and trivially extensible when a fifth journey arrives.

The most practical and the least atmospheric. Worth choosing if journeys are something people sign up for rather than browse — an index with durations answers "which one can I actually do" faster than any photograph.
