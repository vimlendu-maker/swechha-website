Yamunotri → Agra

Following one river from the snow it starts in to the plains it ends up serving — and meeting the people living along every stretch of it.

Mar 202323rd to 30th
6stops along the route

To confirm: the dates come from the archive's own filenames (IMG_20230323…20230330). Total distance is deliberately absent — it was never recorded, so it is not shown.

The journey begins

Day 1
23 Mar

Where the river begins

Snow, rock and a temple. At the source the Yamuna is cold, fast and clear — the only stretch of the whole route where all three are true at once.

Day 2
24 Mar

Walking out of the mountains

The valley widens. Terraced fields, first bridges, first towns — and the first places where something is put into the river rather than taken from it.

Turning point · Day 3

The last clean stretch

Somewhere along here the river stops being something you can drink and becomes something you have to treat. No sign marks the spot. That is the whole problem.

Move through the journey

Day 5
27 Mar

The plains take over

Canals, pumps, brick kilns. The river is now a resource with owners, and every kilometre has a claim on it.

Day 7
29 Mar

Meeting the people who live with it

Fishers, farmers, priests, sand miners, schoolchildren. Everyone downstream has a version of the same sentence: it was not like this before.

Turning point · Day 8

Where the city takes over

The stretch that gives the Yamuna its reputation — and the one most people mean when they say the river is dead. It is not dead. It is being killed in a specific place, by specific things.

Evidence along the way

What we carried back was not data. It was testimony.

No instruments went on this Yatra, so this page reports what was seen and said rather than what was measured — field notes, photographs and interviews along the route. Where a figure is needed, it comes from a named published source on the Yamuna campaign page, never from the journey itself.
