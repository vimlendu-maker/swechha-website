The system

Two families, four voices, three hues with one job each, one structural device, one drawn record and one animation. Every board is assembled from what is on this page and nothing else.

Three deliberate voids: nothing between 23px and 44px, nothing between 44px and 74px, nothing between 104px and 269px. Archivo and Newsreader, both SIL OFL, zero licence cost. Self-host and preload in production.

Three hues,
one job each

The three cannot compete because they are not the same part of speech. Mustard is the second person: you, do this. Red is the third person present: the city is failing. Green is the past perfect: this was brought back.

The band rule. One hue is active per section band, never two. Red and green may not appear in the same band or in adjacent bands. Mustard is the only hue permitted to travel between bands, and it is never permitted inside a data component.

Every value below is lifted from app/globals.css. Nothing was re-picked because the ground changed.

Mustard

Mustard marks a human act.

Anything Swechha does, or anything the reader is being asked to do. It is therefore the interactive colour: primary buttons, links, hover, active, focus, the Give chip, form submits, filter chips. It is the warm register the current site already has.

Never

Never on a reading, a value, a band, a chart or a source line. Never as a general brand tint on a page where nobody is being asked to do anything.

--mustard #E1A32B 8.78:1 on ground  /  --yellow #F1C33B 11.68:1 small text on dark  /  --mustard-ink #8A6410 4.72:1 on paper  /  --on-mustard #12110F 8.51:1

Red

Red means a published legal limit has been broken.

Applied by a comparison, not by a designer: the stored value against the stored limit. It appears in exactly four places. The rail beside a reading. The verdict word. The top band of a scale. The multiplier. Plus the one object inside a photograph in a reading band, through the selective-colour filter.

Never

Never a button, never a link, never a control of any kind. A red Give button teaches a reader that red means "click me", and three screens later red has to mean the river is dead. The single exception is a form field in an error state, which is not a control being coloured but a reading of one: the field is out of limit.

--sev #F1484E 5.35:1 on ground  /  --signal-critical #c81e3a 4.98:1 on paper

Green

Green counts what came back.

Past-tense recovery, and only that. Tonnes out of the river, gardens established, saplings standing, restored ground. It reaches the interface wherever the interface is about recovery: the Recovered tag, the impact figures, and a secondary button that leads to what came back. That is still past perfect, so the rule holds.

Never

Never a projection, a goal or an aspiration. Never on a live reading, even a good one, because a good reading today is not a recovery.

--signal-nature-bright #5FBE85 8.51:1 on ground  /  --signal-nature darkened to #1F6B45 for paper text, since the base is 4.43:1 and fails AA

The controls

Every interactive element at real size, on both grounds, with the token named beside it. Mustard is the interactive colour throughout. Red never becomes a control. Green reaches the interface only where the interface is about recovery.

Tab through this panel. Focus is visible on every element on both grounds, and the primary button inverts its ring so a mustard outline is never drawn on mustard.

And this

The rail

A full-height vertical rule with a giant numeral crossing it. The numeral is pulled past the column boundary by its own right padding plus a quarter of an em, so every value crosses whether it reads 68 or 6,890 or "14 of 18".

Quiet: a 1px rule over the numeral. Breach: the same rule swells to a 6px red band under it. One component, two states, and that swelling is the only way red enters a layout.

41.8

Quiet. Inside the limit

The IMD alert is at 45.0 and this is below it, so there is no colour anywhere in this component.

412

4.1× the limit

Breach. The limit is broken

The multiplier is the same rule expressed as a figure, so the finding survives for a reader who cannot see the red at all.

6,890t

Recovered. Out of the river

Identical component, green label. The only thing that differs between a reading and a recovery is the hue of the word beside it, which is the whole colour argument compressed into one component.

Photography

Black and white, without exception. Two ramps and nothing else: duo for a frame on open ground, duo-dim for a frame under type, bounded at the top end so white copy keeps roughly 5.5:1 on the lightest pixel it can produce.

Selective colour is retired from photography. Hue now lives only in type, data, marks and controls. This is a simplification, not a loss: there is no longer a per-image decision to make at ingest, nothing to decay across a twenty-six-year archive, and the three-hue grammar is untouched because it never depended on the photographs.

Halftone on full-bleed heroes only. Every other frame is clean monochrome. A dot screen everywhere turns a busy mid-tone frame to mud and buys nothing on a thumbnail. The page-wide grain layer is gone.

duo + headline scrim + halftone  /  picture bands only

Bounded ramp, two-axis scrim, 6px dot screen. The only place the halftone appears.

duo  /  everywhere else

Open ramp, no dots, no scrim. Work index, journeys rack, contact sheet, cut-outs.

The cut-out
spine

The connective device across Journeys, the twenty-six-year timeline and How Change Happens. Real archive photographs with their backgrounds removed, arranged down the rail, with the years as the display numerals. Nothing new was invented: the spine is the rail component and the year is the readout role, pointed at a third job.

It replaces the hand-drawn line, which was cut. The drawing was an invention; this is the organisation’s own material, which is the right register for a body of work whose whole claim is that it keeps evidence. It also answers the missing-photography complaint a second time.

Named rule: mustard silhouettes on an ink spine. The reference uses red for both, and we cannot. Red means a published limit has been broken, so a red silhouette behind a history section would be decorative red, which the grammar forbids outright. A timeline of what Swechha and its people did is second person made past, a record of human acts, and mustard is the hue for a human act. Green is reserved for the entries that are genuinely restoration. Red never appears in this device at all.

Production requirement

Cut-outs need frames whose subject separates cleanly from its background: a tractor, a single figure, a sapling, an animal, a tool. They are delivered as transparent PNGs from the archive, not produced in CSS. These boards stand in for that with clip-path, which is honest about the silhouette but cannot cut around an arm.

When no clean cut-out exists

A crowd, a landscape, a scanned poster. In a twenty-six-year archive there will be many. The entry is never skipped and never gets a bad cut-out: it keeps a plain halftoned square frame, and the row is meant to read as different. 2007 above is that case.

Ground,
grid
and chrome

Dark leads and alternates. The page ground is #0D0D0B, the alternate dark band #151512, and off-white is used sparingly: long reads and the occasional break in a long dark scroll. It is relief, not a second ground. Severity is no longer signalled by the ground at all, since dark is now the default rather than the breach state. The rail, the multiplier, the band counter and the word carry it, which is a cleaner system than a ground that flips.

Container 1580px for indexes and instruments, 1240px for reading. Section padding clamp(44px, 5.6vw, 88px), tighter than v2 on purpose. Corner radius is zero everywhere except a 2px tag. No shadow anywhere. Navigation is one line, 62px, and never wraps; under 940px it becomes a scrolling index strip rather than a hamburger.

Motion

One behaviour animates by itself. Everything else responds to a finger. Anything that cannot name its job is not built.

Block arrival

opacity 0 to 1, translateY 14px to 0. 700ms, cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1). IntersectionObserver, once, unobserved after firing. Stagger 70ms across the first four siblings and no further.

Why
Communicates arrival order. It is the only thing on the site that animates by itself.

Slider change

scrollLeft assignment on the track. No scripted animation, no scroll-behavior on the track, so it lands instantly on a button press and stays native under a thumb.

Why
A slide change is a state change, not a journey. Smooth scrolling here costs frames on a mid-range phone and buys nothing the swipe does not already give.

Index row hover

background and underline only, 160ms. Photographs and orders lift nothing and shadow nothing.

Why
Feedback. A row that is a link has to say so.

Layer toggle

attribute change, no transition.

Why
State. A map layer is on or off; fading it implies an in-between that does not exist.

Reduced motion

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce disables the arrival transition entirely and renders every block in place.

Why
Non-negotiable.

Never

No parallax, no pinned sections, no scroll hijack, no marquee, no counters counting up, no custom cursor, no scroll listener anywhere on the site. The selective-colour SVG filters are never animated and never applied to a moving element: they re-rasterise the entire frame every tick and that is what makes a mid-range Android feel broken.

Why
Performance is a design decision, and this site is read on a 4G phone.
