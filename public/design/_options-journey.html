Journey page — three options

Same design language as the homepage: same palette, same three type voices, monochrome photography, mustard used once per screen. What differs is the structure of the narrative. §7 asks for route, beginning, progression, evidence, the larger question and where it leads — all three options carry those, in a different order of priority. Abbreviated to the first few bands each, so the direction is legible without reading a whole page.

Option A

The route as a spine

The page opens on the route set as type — YAMUNOTRI → AGRA larger than the journey's own name, because on a journey page the route is the headline. Below it, a single hairline runs down the left margin with distance markers, and each stop hangs off it as a chapter. Scrolling reads as travelling down the river.

Closest to the homepage's own habits: hairline structure, condensed data voice for the numbers, one accent. Cheapest to build and the safest on mobile — the spine simply straightens into a list.

Option B

Chapters as full-bleed frames

Each stop is its own edge-to-edge photographic frame, at a fixed height, with a stop number and three lines of type held on the dark left third. A thin mustard progress rail sticks to the top of the viewport as you move down. The page becomes a sequence of places rather than a document about a journey.

The most cinematic, and the closest to what §7 means by "the scroll should feel like progression". The cost is real: it needs one strong photograph per stop — six good frames, not four — and it carries the least room for evidence and data.

Option C

Map-led, evidence-forward

A schematic route rail across the top — six stops, distances, the current one marked — that doubles as navigation: click a stop and the page moves to it. Below, each stop pairs one photograph with what was actually measured or observed there, so the journey reads as fieldwork rather than travelogue.

The strongest fit if the Yatra produced data — water readings, drain counts, interviews. It is also the only option that makes the route usable as navigation, which matters if the page gets long. Weakest as pure atmosphere, and it needs the numbers to exist.
