  • The work /work Everything Swechha runs, in one place. Projects, campaigns, journeys and events.
  • Journeys — school camps and walks /work/journeys A thousand kilometres on one river, or five days in a village.
  • CityScapes /work/journeys/cityscapes The eco-walks, renamed. A river bank, a landfill, a ridge forest, a heritage water system — all of it already inside Delhi, all of it reachable before lunch.
  • Gram Anubhav /work/journeys/gram-anubhav Purely experiential, which here means you are not shown a village. You stay in one, eat in one, and do a day's work in one.
  • NatureScapes /work/journeys/naturescapes Six destinations, four ecosystems, one school group at a time. None of the six explains itself from a classroom, which is the only reason to take a bus that far.
  • Yamuna Yatra /work/journeys/yamuna-yatra From where it originates and is pristine, down to the point where it reaches Agra and is almost a toxic body of water. Twelve days.
  • Projects /work/projects Curriculum, campus and garden. The work that runs for years.
  • Bridge the Gap /work/projects/bridge-the-gap Between a hundred and a hundred and fifty Delhi schools every year. Land, water, air, waste, climate change — and who gets left out of all five.
  • Eco Action /work/projects/eco-action One public park in Vasant Kunj went from five per cent green cover to ninety, over a decade. That is the whole argument for this project.
  • Farm School /work/projects/farm-school An organic, experiential, deconstructed un'education Learning Lab. In practice: five acres, an hour and a half from Delhi, and a farm that has to actually produce or the lesson is wrong.
  • Influence /work/projects/influence Ten fellows a year, each one running at a problem in their own neighbourhood. Climate, air, environment, waste. Pick one, then build something.
  • ME to WE /work/projects/me-to-we It started in 2007 as a volunteer school on the banks of the Yamuna. Some of the children who came to it are now core team members here.

Healthy Cities

  • Bridge the Gap — Healthy Cities /healthy-cities What a city’s air, water, waste and food do to the health of the people in it — taught for a year in twenty-six Delhi-NCR schools, and piloted by ten funded fellows.
  • Anjali Choudhary — composting in Bharuch /healthy-cities/fellows/anjali-choudhary Six hundred and seventy-seven people in Bharuch found out where their wet waste goes. Discarded chemical tanks became bulk composters, and one site now takes 180 to 200 kilos a day that used to leave for the landfill.

  • Shubham Panwar — mushrooms in Uttarkashi /healthy-cities/fellows/shubham-panwar Forty women in two Uttarkashi villages, four hundred kilos of straw that would otherwise have been burnt, and seventy kilos of mushrooms out of the first cycle at Saini.   • Swapnil Chaurasiya — waste segregation in Bilaspur /healthy-cities/fellows/swapnil-chaurasiya Almost every child in Bilaspur already thought segregation was a good idea. Doing it at home went from 24 per cent to 80. The nudge was the child, working on the parent.

  • Tanuz Kalita — riverbank saplings in Majuli /healthy-cities/fellows/tanuz-kalita Majuli loses land to the Brahmaputra every year. Three thousand native saplings went into the erosion-prone banks, and fifty kilos of weaving thread went to the households that live on them.
