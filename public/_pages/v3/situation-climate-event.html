Who it kills

3,594 people in 2024, from five named causes in one official table. Four of the five rose.

3,594

deaths from flood, landslide, lightning, torrential rain and cyclone, 2024

361 + 39 + 2825 + 351 + 18 for 2024; 266 + 61 + 2560 + 239 + 2 for 2023. Addition on five published rows of one table, nothing else.

2,825

of them from lightning alone

More than the other four causes put together (769). Lightning is the largest single killer in India’s whole table of deaths from forces of nature, and it is almost never the subject of a climate story.

Landslide deaths rose 46.9% and flood deaths 35.7% in one year. Cyclone deaths went from 2 to 18 — a small number moving a long way, which is what a single landfall looks like in an annual table.

These are recorded deaths, and they are a floor. A death enters this table only if an authority attributed it to a named cause and filed it. And 1,626 deaths in the same table — 20.6% — have no named cause at all.

There is no national count of landslide EVENTS, flash floods or cloudbursts. NCRB counts deaths by cause; nobody publishes how many times the ground moved. So this page can tell you how many people a landslide killed and not how many landslides there were.

What counts as extreme

What counts as extreme

IMD classifies a rainfall day, and the boundaries are its own. So an extreme rainfall day is a countable event, not an adjective.

64.5heavymm in 24 hours
115.6very heavymm in 24 hours
204.5extremely heavymm and above

Every “extreme rain day” on this page is a day that crossed 64.5 mm at that grid point. Nothing here is a judgement about whether the rain was unusual — it is a comparison against a notified boundary.

IMD classifies a DAY, not a storm. 200 mm in three hours and 200 mm spread over a full day are the same category and are not the same event. That distinction is why a day-class cannot tell you whether a street flooded.

Not just the monsoon

Extreme-rain days are counted over the WHOLE YEAR, not just the monsoon. Chennai's extreme rain is the northeast monsoon in October and November, and a June-September window would miss it entirely. The monsoon total is reported separately per station.

The monsoon total is still reported for every city, and the season-to-date figure is compared with the same calendar dates in the 1991-2020 normal — never with a whole-season normal, which always makes a season look dry.

Three things, named rather than estimated

  1. CLOUDBURSTS ARE NOT COUNTED. IMD defines one as 100 mm or more in a single hour. ERA5 is a ~9 km reanalysis that smooths convection, so a real cloudburst appears in it as an ordinary wet hour — counting them here would return roughly zero everywhere, and that zero would be a fact about the instrument, not about India. There is also no public national cloudburst register to check against.
  2. FLASH FLOODS ARE NOT COUNTED for the same reason: there is no public national register of flash flood events, and rainfall alone does not determine whether one happens — terrain, land use and drainage do.
  3. LANDSLIDE COUNTS are not published nationally either. NCRB publishes landslide DEATHS, which is a different quantity and is used instead, from its own table.
  4. India's official rainfall departures are IMD's, computed on its own gauge network by subdivision. These are computed here from a reanalysis and are not IMD's figures.

A zero from the wrong instrument is not a zero. A 9 km reanalysis asked how many 100 mm hours India had would answer “almost none”, and that answer would be about the model, not the country.

What is changing

What is changing

More rain, in more extreme days. The concentration is the part that is not moving.

Four measures, the first eighteen years against the last, counted across all 12 cities. The pale bar is cities where it rose, the grey where it did not move, the dark where it fell.

Total rainfall 9↑ 3↓
Days over the heavy threshold 8↑ 3↓
Very heavy days and above 5↑ 5↓
Share falling in the five wettest days 4↑ 8↓

Rainfall is up at 9 of 12 cities and extreme days at 8. But the share arriving in the five wettest days is down at 8 of them — so this dataset shows more rain and more extreme days without showing rain getting more concentrated. Both things are on the page because both are what it measured.

Counted as cities, never averaged. How many of the reporting stations move each way between the first and second halves of the record. Counted as stations, never averaged into a national trend. And ERA5 reanalysis — a model constrained by observations. Rain is far more local than heat: one gauge can record a cloudburst another two kilometres away never sees, and a 9 km cell has one answer.

Coverage follows the flood, not the rainfall. A 300 mm day in a place with drainage is a wet afternoon. The same day where a lake was built over is a disaster. The rain is the same; only one of them gets a headline — and it is the second one that tells you something about a city.

What you can do

What you can do

Rainfall is weather. A flood is a decision.

Find out what was there before

Most urban flooding happens where water used to go — a lake, a nala, a floodplain. City master plans and old survey maps are public. If your street floods, something was built on the way out.

The Yamuna floodplain

Lightning is the one you can act on today

2,825 deaths a year, mostly outdoors, mostly avoidable with thirty minutes of shelter. It is the largest killer on this page and the cheapest to reduce.

What we work on

Ask who signed it off

Every building on a floodplain has an approval with a name on it. That is a public record, and it is a more useful question than how much it rained.

Support the work

Every figure here is public, dated and reproducible from the source named beside it — and the three things that could not be counted are named where they would have gone.

Every reading, kept

Each reading keeps its own address, with the source that produced it, when it was observed, and the limit it was judged against. Nothing is overwritten when it improves and nothing is quietly restated when it gets worse. An empty day stays empty — a gap in the record is a gap in the record, never a zero.

And why it is not a reply

Publishing and going to see are not resilience. Swechha does not build drainage, and nothing here reduces the 3,594 deaths this page counts. What the work does is make the count harder to ignore — a smaller claim than it sounds, and the only one the evidence supports.
