15 of 30 states and union territories have at least one city above the limit. A state with three cities in the feed and three above the limit is not the same finding as a state with forty and three, which is why the count of cities is beside it. Nothing here is a state average: CPCB measures cities, not states, and the unmeasured parts of a state are unmeasured, not clean.

How to read a rank

Four things decide whether a row above means what it looks like it means. Three of them are about who is holding the monitor.

The number is one machine

A city’s rank here is its worst monitor. A city with forty monitors has forty chances to produce a high reading; a city with one has one. That cuts against the well-measured city: 199 of these 261 cities are a single station wearing a city’s name, and a city with one monitor is measured less, not better. Read the station count before you read the rank.

Two numbers, two questions

“Worst” is the worst station. “Mean” is the average across the city’s stations — the figure CPCB itself publishes for that city. Against CPCB’s own published figures this ranking runs about a quarter higher. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions, so both are on the row.

The scale is not a ratio

The AQI is piecewise-linear. 200 is not twice the pollution of 100 — it is twice the index. Nothing on this page multiplies one AQI by another, and neither should a sentence written from it.

Every pollutant counts

A station’s AQI is its worst published sub-index, whichever pollutant that is. Where the governing pollutant is not particulate the row names it, because a rank is not comparable unless you can see what is being ranked. Nothing is excluded: CPCB publishes sub-indexes, already on one scale.

4 rows carry a question mark. At Dhanbad, Patna, Chennai, Leh one gas channel reads far above the worst particulate at the same station and no independent monitor is near enough to say which is right. They are ranked on particulates; the gas figure is published in the tooltip, not ranked. Hover or focus the mark to read it.

These are the cities CPCB measures, not the cities of India. A place with no monitor produces no row, and an absent row is an absent instrument — never clean air. 261 cities is the whole of the national real-time network.

There is no state figure here and there will not be one. CPCB measures cities; averaging a state’s cities would invent a reading for the land between them.

A failed fetch leaves the previous hour in place rather than writing a zero, so a stale hour is possible and is printed on the page. The hour above is the only claim about freshness this page makes.
