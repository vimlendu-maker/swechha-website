## 1. What is structurally wrong today

Bands 03–05 are not a structure, they are a residue. WORK is the umbrella for
four kinds of thing — Projects, Journeys, Campaigns, Events — by the owner's
own ruling of 19 August, which is written into `components/site-nav.tsx` and
shipped in `redirects.ts` when `/campaigns` moved to `/work/campaigns`. The
homepage gives one of those four a full band of its own, gives two of them a
shared list whose own lead sentence miscounts them, and gives the fourth
nothing at all. Meanwhile the statement band, which the art direction calls
"the hinge of the whole page", hinges onto nothing: it delivers an aphorism
about the Yamuna and lands on a band about walking routes, so the organisation
never actually enters. A reader at 375 goes from *here are the readings* to
*here are four walks* across 2,532px without once being told who is doing
this, or what they do, or that there is more than walking.

---

## 2. Was the promotion of Journeys decided? No. It is a reverted decision that came back.

This is the client's actual question and it has an evidence answer, not an
opinion answer.

`components/site-nav.tsx:9–17`, verbatim in the file:

> *Owner ruling, 19 Aug: WORK is the umbrella and its dropdown holds Projects,
> Journeys, Campaigns and Events. That also settles the conflict between the
> ledger's flat seven-item nav and the journey mockups, which drew JOURNEYS as
> its own top-level item.*

`home.html:2112` — the v3 header, built on 20 August:

> `Now · Work · Journeys · Impact · Farm · Record`

That is a flat nav with JOURNEYS as a sibling of WORK: **precisely the
arrangement the 19 August ruling overturned, reinstated in a file written the
next day.** The homepage band structure follows the header, not the ruling. So
the promotion is not a decision anyone took about Journeys' importance — it is
the journey mockups' navigation surviving into v3, and the band order was built
to match it.

That does not make the promotion *wrong*. It makes it **unexamined**, which is
what the client suspected. Option C below is the version where it is examined
and kept.

### Two more facts that bear on it

- **The count in band 05 is wrong on the page as it stands.** The lead says
  *"Five projects and five campaigns"*; the ledger under it holds eight items —
  measured tags in DOM order: `Campaign, Project, Campaign, Project, Project,
  Project, Campaign, Project` = **3 campaigns and 5 projects**. Two campaigns
  the sentence promises are not there. Whichever option is chosen, this is a
  fact the client has to settle: is it five campaigns or three?
- **Neither `journey` nor `event` exists as a content type.** `lib/content/types.ts`
  declares exactly five: `project, story, knowledge, film, campaign`. So the
  four things the nav promises are two content types, one design-only construct
  (Journeys) and one thing with no representation anywhere (Events). Any option
  that puts all four on the homepage is also a request for two new content types.

### OPTION A — one WHAT WE DO index, and the running order stays
`/design/v3/_ad3/a.html` · `ad3-opt-a-1440-top.png`, `ad3-opt-a-375-top.png`

The client's literal proposal. Band 03 is untouched. Band 04 stops being
Journeys and becomes a four-column index — a numeral, a rule, an account, which
is the grammar the rest of the page already speaks: **5 Projects · 4 Journeys ·
3 Campaigns · — Events**, each column listing its actual items by name. Band 05
stays exactly as it is.

The Events column carries an em-dash where a numeral belongs, and a hatched
panel reading *"No events published yet. The count stays empty until one is."*
That is the archive sheet's own grammar (`.s-record-off`) reused, so the hole
shows rather than being hidden — which the through-line requires.

**Gains** — the umbrella is stated once, in the organisation's own words,
before anything is listed. It matches the nav and the sitemap. Events exist on
the page for the first time. The reader learns the shape of the organisation in
one screen.

**Losses** — the four journey photographs leave the homepage, and so does *"You
cannot argue someone into caring about a river"*, the best headline on the page.
Both would move to `/work/journeys`, which is already designed and locked, so
this is a move rather than a loss of work — but it is a real loss of warmth from
the homepage, and it is the client's own stated risk: a Yamuna Yatra and a
poster campaign end up as equal-weight rows in equal-weight columns.

**Cost** — moderate. New band, ~60 lines of CSS, two blocks of approved copy
relocated to a locked page.

**New copy required** — a headline for the band. I have set *"Four kinds of
thing"* and a lead *"Five projects, four journeys, three campaigns, and the
events we have not written up yet."* Both are **proposals**, both need approval.

### OPTION B — the statement becomes the hinge it is supposed to be
`/design/v3/_ad3/b.html` · `ad3-opt-b-1440-top.png`, `ad3-opt-b-375-top.png`

The photograph and the sentence stay exactly as they are, at full height. Under
them, inside the same band, one hairline and then the four-kind index at
micro-scale on solid ground: **5 Projects · 4 Journeys · 3 Campaigns · — Events**,
and one mustard control, *What is running →*. That is the ticker's caging
grammar reused, so it reads as instrument chrome rather than as a second
headline. Band 04 then becomes a single *What is running* on paper covering all
four kinds — twelve rows plus the Events hole — and band 05 dissolves into it.

This is the option that answers the client's sentence most literally. *"This is
where the Swechha bit starts"* — in B, it starts inside band 03, at ≈950px, on
the same screen as the sentence, and the band finally earns the word "hinge":
the sentence turns the data into a body and the strip immediately says whose
body and what they do.

**Gains** — the statement keeps its full-viewport moment, its halftone and its
place in the ground rhythm; nothing in the closed list moves. The organisation
enters 1,350px earlier than today. A whole band disappears. The journeys' mobile
breach disappears with it — there is no journeys band left to breach.

**Losses** — the four journey photographs shrink to 52–72px row thumbnails, and
at ≤767 they vanish entirely (the row loses its picture so thirteen rows fit in
a phone screen's worth of scroll). The contact-sheet hook the art direction gave
band 04 — *"black-and-white photographs printed on off-white, reading as a
contact print"* — is gone. That is a genuinely good idea being spent.

**Still over budget** — `run` is 1,029.3px at 375 against the 900 cap. Closing
it needs the head group tightened or the ledger paged; I have not done either,
so the number you see is honest.

**Cost** — moderate-to-high. Paper variant of the ledger (~40 lines), the strip
(~25 lines), band 05 deleted, band 04 rebuilt.

**New copy required** — none. Every string is moved, not written.

### OPTION C — keep the promotion, and make it honest
`/design/v3/_ad3/c.html` · `ad3-opt-c-1440-top.png`, `ad3-opt-c-375-top.png`

Journeys keeps its band, its four photographs and its headline. What changes is
that the band stops pretending to be a peer of Work: its eyebrow becomes
**"Work · Journeys"**, which states the relation using the IA's own words and no
new copy. The two paragraphs leave the band (they belong to the locked Journeys
page), which takes it from 1,130.9 to 1,059.6 at 1440 and from 1,158.7 to 974.6
at 375. Band 05 gains an Events row rendered as a visible hole in the ledger, on
the same hatch as the archive sheet.

**Gains** — cheapest by a wide margin, and it keeps the four best photographs
and the best headline on the homepage. If the client's honest answer is *yes,
journeys really is the thing we are known for*, this is the right option and the
others are over-engineering.

**Losses** — the nav/sitemap contradiction survives; a reader who opens WORK in
the header finds Journeys inside it, having just seen Journeys as a band of its
own. The four-way umbrella is still never stated on the homepage — the reader is
shown one kind, then a list of two more, and has to infer the set. Events remain
a single hole rather than a named part of the offer. And it moves almost nothing:
**−117px at 375**, journeys still 74.6px over cap.

**Cost** — low. An eyebrow, a copy move, one ledger row.

**New copy required** — none.

### OPTION D — no statement band; its frame titles what we do
`/design/v3/_ad3/d.html` · `ad3-opt-d-1440-top.png`, `ad3-opt-d-1440-2.png`, `ad3-opt-d-375-top.png`

The statement stops being a band and becomes the **frame** of the what-we-do
band: same photograph, same halftone, same sentence, at 468px instead of 702
(357.3 instead of 503.4 at 375), with the four-kind index on paper directly
beneath it. Bands 04 and 05 both dissolve into that one band.

The halftone count is unaffected — the definition in `groundRhythm` is *"a
photograph that runs edge to edge, carries a display headline inside its frame,
and has no other content in that frame"*, which this still satisfies, exactly as
the farm band does with its `.pic` + `.pic-body`. It stays 3 of 3.

**Gains** — the arithmetic is not close: **−1,459px at 1440, −1,275px at 375,
and the whole document lands at 8,183px**, inside the doctrine's ≈8,200 target,
with every band in 03–05 under the 900 cap. The organisation enters at ≈950px.
And the aphorism stops being an aphorism: instead of a beautiful band that
delivers a line and moves on, the line becomes the title of the thing it
introduces, which is what a headline is for. This is the option a full AI-tells
audit would arrive at on its own.

**Losses** — three designed things, and they should be said plainly:
1. `groundRhythm`'s **instrument triad** — *"a reading, more readings, and the
   sentence about readings"* — loses its third leg. Paper now arrives after two
   dark bands instead of three.
2. The page's **two full-viewport moments** become one. The statement was
   explicitly specced *"deliberately shorter than the hero so the two are not
   twins"*; in D there is no second one to be a twin of.
3. The halftone's **placement rhythm** — *"roughly at 0%, 25% and 70% down the
   page"* — becomes roughly 0%, 12% and 60%. The count survives; the spacing
   does not.

**Cost** — high in deletion, low in construction: one band removed, one band
rebuilt, ~50 lines of CSS. It is the biggest change to the approved art
direction and it needs the client to accept those three costs explicitly.

**New copy required** — the eyebrow/headline pair in the built version reads
*"What we do"* over *"What is running"*, which is a redundancy I would fix
before shipping; one of the two goes. Otherwise none.

## 4. Things worth knowing before you choose

**The page already introduces Swechha — two bands further down.** Band 06, the
timeline, opens *"Swechha means, roughly, of one's own free will."* over
*"Nobody was assigned to the river. Everyone who has ever cleaned it turned up."*
That is the introduction, and it is very good. It is at 4,900px at 1440 today.
If the client's real want is "the Swechha bit starts here", the cheapest possible
answer is not in this document at all — it is to move that opening pair up. I
have not built it because it reorders the ground sequence and takes band 06's
pause with it, but it deserves saying before anyone spends a band on the problem.

**"What we do" before the situations was considered and rejected.** Putting the
index above the ticker would mean the page opens by describing itself, and D-01.12
has already ruled against exactly that shape at the top of the page ("no genre
label… the statement band one screen down already owns that job"). Leaving it
where it is also keeps the ground alternation intact; moving it above the ticker
would put paper at band 02 and force a re-cost of all twelve grounds.

**All four options leave the header wrong.** Whatever is chosen, `home.html`'s
nav still lists JOURNEYS beside WORK, contradicting the shipped app. Under P-1
that is not work to do now — but it is the thing that produced this whole
question, and it should be corrected in the same pass that signs the homepage
off, not later.

## 5. My pick, and why — **B**, with D as the honest alternative

**B.** It is the only option that answers the client's own sentence exactly
rather than approximately: Swechha enters *at* band 03, on the same screen as
the sentence, and the band becomes a hinge in fact rather than in the spec. It
costs no new copy, breaks nothing on the closed list, removes a whole band, and
kills the page's worst mobile offender outright instead of trimming it.

**D is better arithmetic and I am not hiding that** — it is the only option that
lands the mobile budget, and it is the more intellectually honest reading of a
band that currently exists to be beautiful. I do not pick it because it spends
three separately designed things (the instrument triad, the second full-viewport
moment, the halftone spacing) to buy an outcome B gets most of the way to. If
the client's priority is the phone, take D and amend the art direction in the
same breath.

**A** is the client's literal proposal and it works, but it pays the four journey
photographs and the page's best headline for a gain of 221px at 375. If the
index is what is wanted, B delivers the same index for free, inside a band that
already exists.

**C** is right only if the answer to Q1 below is "yes". It is otherwise a way of
not deciding.

## 6. Questions only the client can answer

**Q1. Is Journeys the thing Swechha is known for — enough to hold a homepage
band of its own while Projects, Campaigns and Events share one?**
This is the whole fork. *Yes* → Option C, and the nav gets fixed so the header
stops contradicting it. *No, they are four peers* → A, B or D. *It is the thing
we most want to be known for, whether or not we are* → that is a strategy
answer and it also points at C, but it should be said out loud so the next
person does not re-open it.

**Q2. Five campaigns or three?** The homepage sentence says five, the page shows
three, and the CMS holds one. I need the true number, and the names of the two
missing campaigns if they exist. Every option renders this count; none can render
it honestly until you answer.

**Q3. Do Events exist yet — and are they events *we run* or events *we turn up
to*?** All four options render Events as a visible, hatched hole, which is the
site's grammar for a record not yet filled. That is only right if the honest
answer is "yes, we run them, they are not written up". If the honest answer is
"we do not really run events", the fourth item should come off the nav rather
than onto the homepage as a hole, and the umbrella becomes three.

**Q4. Would you spend the statement band?** Option D deletes a full-viewport
moment that the approved art direction designed deliberately, and buys the
mobile budget with it. I would not spend it. But it is a proprietor's call, not
an art director's, because what it really trades is one moment of stillness
against roughly one and a half phone screens of scrolling on the platform most
of your readers use.

## 7. What I would not change, in any option

- **The photograph and the sentence.** Both survive in all four options, and the
  sentence works in every one of them. Nothing here is an argument for a
  different frame or a different line.
- **The four-kind index as a numeral, a rule and an account.** It is not a new
  pattern — it is the hero rail and the archive tally applied to the
  organisation's own remit, and it is the reason the Events hole reads as
  candour rather than as an error.
- **The Events hole itself.** Every instinct will be to hide an empty column.
  The through-line exists to stop that.
- **The journeys' horizontal snap rail at ≤767.** It is already built and it
  works; options that keep the band keep the rail verbatim.
- **The spine.** Hero, statement and journeys all sit at x = 146 at 1440
  (measured; only the ticker steps out to 46, correctly, as chrome). Every
  option preserves it. Do not let a new band introduce a second left edge.
