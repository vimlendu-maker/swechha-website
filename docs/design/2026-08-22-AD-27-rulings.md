# A · NAVIGATION

The client asked for certainty and asked for an expert. One was consulted from outside the
build. Their verdict is adopted whole, and it is recorded here with the reasoning so that
the next session does not reopen it.

## AD-27.1 · Nav clicks go to the real page, from everywhere including the homepage. The scroll-spy underline is RETIRED, not revived.

**The decision.** Every nav word navigates to its own route from every page, the homepage
included; the mustard 2px rule under a nav word means *"this page belongs to this
chapter"* and never *"your eye is here"*.

**Why.** Five reasons, in the order of how much they cost:
1. **SEO is decisive.** Google treats `/#work` as a link to `/`. A homepage-anchor nav
   therefore gives the five chapter pages **no internal links from the site's most-linked
   page** — the exact opposite of what §H is trying to buy.
2. **Shareability.** `/#work` re-downloads a 10,900px homepage and drops the reader in the
   middle of a document they did not ask for.
3. **The back button.** An anchor nav writes a history entry per click, so Back walks the
   reader up their own scroll history instead of out of the page.
4. **Screen readers.** A route change moves focus and re-announces the title. An anchor
   jump usually does neither.
5. **Analytics.** Fragments never reach the server, so a homepage-anchor nav is invisible
   in any log the owner will ever read.

**The hybrid is rejected, and this is the part worth keeping.** Keeping the scroll-spy
underline while the clicks navigate gives **one mark two meanings**: the identical 2px
mustard rule already means "you are inside this chapter" on every inner page, and the
second meaning would be *a mark that lies about where the click goes*. And because the nav
names five of the homepage's fourteen bands, an honest scroll-spy goes dark nine times on
the way down — which reads as flicker, not as orientation. **The underline going dark in
nine bands is not a bug that better code fixes; it is the true statement that the nav is a
selection of five destinations, not a partition of fourteen bands** (BRANDING §5.10 already
says so).

**Supersedes D-09.4** (*"Wire the active-section underline, including aria-current"*) for
the homepage only. D-09.4's mechanism — one attribute, CSS already drawn — was correct
engineering for a nav that pointed at bands. AD-23 rewrote every nav href to a canonical
route and the mechanism stopped matching anything; this ruling closes it deliberately
rather than leaving it dead.

**What changes.** See AD-27.2 (the deletion) and AD-27.3 (the table).

## AD-27.2 · The dead D-09.4 IIFE and its commentary are DELETED. The CSS stays.

**The decision.** Delete the scroll-spy IIFE and the block comment that explains it from
`public/_pages/v3/home.html` and from every shell that reproduces it. Keep
`.nav a.nl[aria-current]{border-bottom-color:var(--mustard)}` and every rule around it.

**Why.** The IIFE selects `.nav a.nl[href*="#"]` and, since AD-23, that selector matches
**zero elements on all 35 built pages**. It is the single most misleading artefact in the
repository: a reader of the file sees a working active-section system, a reader of the page
sees no underline, and both are correct. BRANDING §7.2 already rules that dead observer
code is left inert only where it is *named* as inert (`.rise`); this one is not named, it
is described as working. The CSS is not dead — AD-23 made it fully used by the static
`aria-current` attributes, which is why it stays and why deleting the JS costs nothing
visible.

**What changes.**
- `public/_pages/v3/home.html` — remove the IIFE and its comment. **Removing lines from
  `home.html` is line-count sensitive: `scripts/lib/situation-shell.mjs` extracts
  `home.html`'s CSS by absolute line range (ranges quoted at `situation-shell.mjs:136–139`,
  and `scripts/build-situation-air.mjs:41,43` pins `R(10,414,…)` and `R(529,840,…)`).**
  The IIFE lives below every pinned range; verify that before deleting, and if any pinned
  range moves, **the generators fail the build loudly rather than emitting a wrong file** —
  that failure is the gate, do not work around it.
- Any copy of the same IIFE inside `scripts/lib/situation-shell.mjs` or
  `scripts/lib/work-shell.mjs` goes with it (grep `aria-current="location"` and
  `IntersectionObserver`).
- **Retain** `if(!('IntersectionObserver' in window)) return;` nowhere — the guard goes
  with the thing it guarded.

**Rejected:** leaving it in place with a comment saying it is dead. This repo has twice
been bitten by code that outlived its selector (`.delayed`, `.rise`); a third instance,
in the chrome layer, on 35 pages, is a liability, and the CSS it existed to trigger is now
driven by markup that cannot drift.

## AD-27.3 · The `aria-current` table — every page, exactly.

**The decision.** The homepage marks the **wordmark**, not a nav word. Chapter indexes mark
their word `page`. Children mark their parent word `true`. The four unmarked pages get
marked.

| standing on | marked element | value | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/` (home) | the **wordmark** `.mark` | `page` | the bar is never wholly unmarked, and the wordmark's href *is* this URL |
| `/now` | `Now` | `page` | href equals URL |
| `/now/<slug>` ×6 | `Now` | `true` | right location, href is the parent |
| `/work` | `Work` | `page` | href equals URL (W-16 reinstated the page) |
| `/work/projects`, `/work/campaigns`, `/work/journeys`, `/work/events` | `Work` | `true` | — |
| `/work/journeys` | **also** `Journeys` | `page` | ~~href equals URL; both words may mark, they are different claims~~ **WITHDRAWN 23 August — see AD-27.3a in §J. One mark per bar: `/work/journeys` marks `Journeys` only.** |
| `/work/journeys/<slug>` ×4 | `Journeys` | `true` | and `Work` **not** marked — two `true`s on one bar is noise |
| `/work/projects/<slug>` ×5, `/work/campaigns/<slug>` ×1 | `Work` | `true` | — |
| `/impact` | `Impact` | `page` | — |
| `/farm` | `Farm` | `page` | — |
| **`/act`** | the **GIVE chip** | `page` | **new** — the chip's href is `/act` |
| **`/search`** | the **search control** | `page` | **new** — if the bar has no search control, mark nothing and say so in the build |
| **`/about`** | nothing | — | **new, and deliberate**: About is not a nav word (AD-27.11 puts it in the footer instead), so marking any word would be false |
| **`/stories`, `/publications`** | nothing | — | same reason |
| `/` cold-loaded at `/#work` etc. | the wordmark | `page` | cold hashes keep working (AD-27.4) |

**Rules the table encodes, so a generator can derive rather than restate it:**
`aria-current="page"` **only** where the marked element's `href` string equals the URL being
written; `"true"` where the word is the correct chapter but its href is the parent; nothing
at all where neither is true. **A page that marks nothing is a legitimate state** and must
not be "fixed" by lighting the nearest word — BRANDING §5.10: pointing `aria-current` at the
wrong section is worse than pointing it nowhere.

**Why the homepage marks the wordmark.** It is the one honest mark available: the wordmark's
href is `/`, the reader is on `/`, and it costs no new CSS because `.mark` can take the same
`[aria-current]` declaration the nav links already carry. This also answers the IA's stated
failure mode (AD-27.5).

## AD-27.4 · Cold `/#work` hashes keep working. No redirect, no rewrite.

**The decision.** `/#work`, `/#farm`, `/#record` and every other homepage fragment continue
to resolve to their band. Nothing redirects them; nothing rewrites them.

**Why.** They are real anchors on a real page, they are in the wild (the footer, body copy,
and any link anyone has already shared), and `scroll-padding-top:var(--nav-h)` already lands
them within ±0.5px. A redirect would break a working URL to enforce a preference.

## AD-27.5 · The stated failure mode, and its mitigation, are on the record.

**The decision.** A reader 8,000px down the homepage sees no nav word lit. That is accepted,
mitigated two ways, and **not** to be solved by re-adding a scroll-spy.

**The mitigation, both parts required:**
1. The wordmark carries `aria-current="page"` on the homepage (AD-27.3), so the bar always
   has exactly one mark.
2. **Every homepage band head must end in its own `→ chapter` link.** Band 4 already carries
   *"The whole list →"* (W-16 kept it). Audit the other thirteen: any band whose subject has
   a page and whose head does not link to it is a defect this ruling opens. This is the same
   requirement §H's internal-linking rule makes for SEO — one change, two reasons.

**If it later proves insufficient**, the only permitted answer is a separate, clearly
labelled **"On this page"** local index, in different type, **with no mustard**, never
borrowing the primary nav's mark. Do not reintroduce the underline.

# B · HOMEPAGE

## AD-27.6 · The AQI flash: the reading arrives twice, and the page says so both times.

> **AMENDED 23 AUGUST — SEE AD-27.6-A IN §J. Clauses 2, 3 and 4 below are SUPERSEDED.**
> Clause 2's premise ("for most readers the flash disappears entirely") was measured and is
> false: nine of ten cold loads repainted the numeral, because `data-committed` is refreshed
> daily and the reading moves hourly. The numeral is now a build artefact that nothing
> repaints, refreshed hourly, and the live fetch only confirms the chip. Clauses 1, 5, 6 and
> 7 stand and are still the rules. The text below is kept as the record of what was aimed at.

**The decision.** Keep the live upgrade. Fix the *arrival*: the first paint states plainly
that it is the committed reading, the upgrade only repaints when the value actually
changed, it cross-fades rather than teleports, it has a deadline, and when it changes the
number it accounts for the number it replaced.

**Why.** This is not a bug — it is D-21.5's designed behaviour ("the page renders the
committed reading and **upgrades**"), and D-21.5 earned `LIVE` with three hours of measured
evidence. But a figure that changes under the reader's eye, silently, in the largest type
on the page, **destroys the one thing this site is built to sell**. The honesty vocabulary
says a reading carries its provenance and its hour (BRANDING §3.4). A reading that quietly
becomes a different reading carries neither. The defect is not the second value; it is that
the first value never admitted what it was and the second never admitted it had replaced
anything.

**The three options, judged:**
- **(ii) a neutral placeholder until the fetch resolves — REJECTED.** It puts a hole where
  the page's whole argument is, it is the worst possible CLS (the largest box on the page
  arrives late), and on fetch failure the reader is left with nothing. And it is dishonest
  in this site's own terms: the record is *not* missing — a sourced, committed reading
  exists. §4.3's "where a value is not known the row does not render" governs unknown
  values, not known-but-older ones.
- **(iii) drop the client fetch, ship the build-time figure with its timestamp — REJECTED,
  but named as the fallback.** It throws away the only genuinely live thing on the site, it
  demotes the badge from LIVE to PERIODIC everywhere, and the client explicitly values the
  realtime figure. **It is, however, the correct emergency state**: if `/api/air` is ever
  removed or its key lapses, this is what the page becomes automatically, with no code
  change, because of clause 5 below.
- **(i) honest committed reading, animate in the live one only when it differs — ADOPTED**,
  with five clauses that make it implementable.

**The mechanism, in full.**

1. **First paint claims nothing it cannot prove.** The static markup carries the committed
   value, the state chip reads **`PERIODIC`**, and the provenance line reads the committed
   observation hour in the site's own form — *"CPCB continuous monitor, Anand Vihar.
   Hourly. Observed 07:00 IST."* **No `LIVE` may appear in static markup on any page**
   (D-10.1, by construction rather than by discipline). If the current markup ships `LIVE`
   statically, that is a D-10.1 violation and this ruling closes it.
2. **No repaint when the value is unchanged.** The numeral carries
   `data-committed="<value>"`. On resolve, `if (String(next) === el.dataset.committed)` the
   digits are **not touched at all** — only the badge and the provenance line update. On an
   hourly feed most page loads land inside the same hour, so **for most readers the flash
   disappears entirely.** This one `if` removes the majority of the reported defect and is
   the cheapest half of this ruling.
3. **When it did change, it cross-fades and it is accounted for.** `opacity 0 → 1` over
   **160ms** (`.16s`, the file's existing transition duration — 18 transitions use it) on
   the numeral only. **No transform, no counting animation, no number-rolling.** Digits are
   `tabular-nums` with the `.dp` decimal slot, so the box width is stable and **CLS is
   zero** — verify at 375 and 1440 by measuring the numeral's rect before and after. Under
   `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` the fade is dropped and the value is set
   instantly. **This adds no keyframe** — the page still has exactly one (`s-hero-live`),
   so BRANDING §7.2's ban on a reveal system is untouched: this is a state change on one
   element, not scroll-triggered motion.
4. **The replaced value is not erased — it is cited.** When and only when the value
   changed, a `.cap` line appears immediately under the provenance line:
   > **Refreshed from CPCB at 14:00 IST. The committed reading on this page was 311.**
   This is the ruling's real content. It converts a glitch into provenance, it is the same
   grammar `/impact` and `/farm` already use for a corrected fact, and it means a reader who
   saw 311 for 300 milliseconds is never left wondering whether the site is unreliable.
   *Copy is fixed; the two figures and the hour substitute.*
5. **The upgrade has a deadline, and failure is silent.** Wrap the fetch in an
   `AbortController` with a **6-second** timeout. After that the page **stays as it is,
   permanently, for that page view.** Reason: a swap that arrives twenty seconds in, while
   the reader is halfway down the method table, is a worse version of the defect being
   fixed. On abort, on a non-200, on `{ ok:false }`, on a parse failure: **change nothing,
   show no error, and `console.warn` once** with the reason. A committed reading correctly
   stamped `PERIODIC` with its own hour is already a complete and honest page. (Warn rather
   than swallow: BRANDING §6.7 — the console guards in the date code are the only thing that
   ever caught a silently-dead IIFE.)
6. **`LIVE` requires a fresh observation, not merely a successful fetch.** Set the badge to
   `LIVE` only when the fetch succeeded **and** the returned observation stamp is within the
   last **two hours**. Otherwise take the new value and **leave the badge on `PERIODIC`**.
   D-21.5's own words are *"LIVE describes the fetch; the age describes the observation"* —
   which is true, and is exactly why a five-hour-old row arriving over a working fetch must
   not be badged live. This is the one refinement of D-21.5 in this pass and it narrows the
   claim rather than widening it.
7. **Cut the window at the edge, not in the client.** `/api/air`'s response must carry
   `Cache-Control: s-maxage=300, stale-while-revalidate=3300`. An hourly feed does not need
   a per-request upstream call, and a 20ms edge hit instead of a 300–2000ms upstream round
   trip is **the single largest reduction in the flash window available**, plus it removes
   most of `data.gov.in`'s measured flakiness (D-21.5: three consecutive calls returned
   200, 200, 502) from the reader's path. If the route is currently `force-dynamic` with
   `no-store`, that is the line to change. Keep `no-store` on `/api/ward*` — those are
   per-reader.

**What the reader sees, stated for each case, because this is what the ruling is for:**

| case | what the reader sees |
|---|---|
| fast connection, value unchanged | the committed number, once. Badge flips PERIODIC → LIVE. **Nothing moves.** |
| fast connection, value changed | the committed number, then a 160ms fade to the new one, then a line saying what it was and when it refreshed |
| slow connection (>6s) | the committed number with `PERIODIC` and its observed hour. Complete, sourced, honest. Nothing spins, nothing blanks, nothing arrives late |
| `/api/air` 503 (no key) | identical to slow. One console warning |
| upstream 502 | identical. One console warning |
| JS off | identical, forever. The page is correct without any script |
| reduced motion | identical to the above, with no fade |

**Where.** `public/_pages/v3/home.html` (hero markup + the inline upgrade script),
`app/api/air/route.ts` (the cache header), and **every other built page that runs the same
upgrade** — the same seven clauses apply verbatim wherever `/api/air` is fetched, including
`situation-air.html`, whose numeral shows the same 311-then-live behaviour. Lane 1 owns
home.html and the route; lane 5 applies clauses 1–6 to the situation set (§I).

## AD-27.12 · The favicon is the Swechha mark, white, on the site's own ground.

**The decision.** The favicon is the **circular mark alone** — not the lockup, not the
wordmark — knocked out in `#FBF8F0` on a solid `#0D0D0B` tile, edge to edge with no extra
padding.

**Why the mark and not the wordmark.** At 16px a 4:1 horizontal lockup renders the wordmark
at roughly one pixel per letter: unreadable, and it makes the tab look like a smudge. The
mark is a circle with a leaf inside it — a silhouette, which is what a favicon is for.

**Why a solid tile and not transparency.** A tab strip is light on one OS setting and dark
on another. A transparent PNG with a black mark vanishes in dark mode; with a white mark it
vanishes in light mode. A solid `#0D0D0B` tile is legible in both, and it is the site's own
ground, so the tab reads as a small piece of the page. **Rejected:** transparency in either
polarity, and a mustard tile (mustard is a ground exactly once on this whole site, at
`#give`, and BRANDING §1.1 says a second mustard field spends what licenses mustard as the
control colour everywhere else — a favicon is a field).

**Where the art comes from, measured, so nobody has to eyeball a crop.** The mark occupies
**exactly 224 × 224 px at origin (79, 147)** inside both
`public/brand/swechha-horizontal-white-approved.png` and
`public/brand/swechha-horizontal-black-approved.png` (both 2048 × 512; alpha bounding box of
the whole lockup is `(79,147)–(1914,371)`, and the first column-run ends at x=302). **Crop
box `(79, 147, 303, 371)`.** Verified on both files; the geometry is identical.

**Honest note on legibility, because it decides the sizes below.** The mark is a fine-line
drawing: at 224px the strokes are roughly 6px, so at 32px they are ~0.9px and at 16px they
are sub-pixel. **At 16px this favicon will read as a circle with something in it, and that
is enough** — a tab is identified by silhouette and colour, not by draughtsmanship. Do not
attempt to thicken the strokes; redrawing the mark is forbidden (BRANDING §2.2: *"The logo
is an asset, never live type… never redraw it"*).

## AD-27.13 · Favicon — the files, and how they are wired into BOTH surfaces.

**The decision.** Six files, one replacement, two wiring points.

**Generate with PIL, not `sips`.** BRANDING §8.4: *"`sips --cropOffset` is unreliable. Crop
with PIL."* Python 3 with Pillow is present on this machine and was used to measure the crop

# C · THE CONTACT DOOR — **the Ask**

> *"So schools would like to contact us, funders contact us, institutions contact us, media
> contact us. Design a better route of communication, it needs to be short turn around,
> no-one wants to read more after they have decided to be a partner, option should come to
> them immediately and an easy one."*

This is the biggest design problem in the brief and it is the one with the clearest answer.
**The reader has already decided. Every additional page is a leak.** Today "Bring your
school" on `/work/projects/bridge-the-gap` and "Book a walk" on
`/work/journeys/cityscapes` both navigate to `/act`, a 10,072px page whose Partner band is
1,527px of reading — which is exactly the wrong thing to hand somebody who has stopped
reading.

## AD-27.14 · The component is called **the Ask**, and it resolves the ask IN PLACE.

**The decision.** One reusable component, `.ask`, replaces the navigating CTA at every point
on the site where a reader can decide to work with Swechha. It is a native `<details>`
whose `<summary>` is the ask itself, styled as the primary button it replaces. Opening it
reveals, on the same page, without a navigation: one sentence about who reads it, one
prefilled email button, the plain address as selectable text, and one quiet link onward to
`/act` for the reader who *does* want to read more.

**Why "the Ask".** The site already calls these things asks — AD-25 §3 counts *"22 named
asks"* and titles a band *"Where an ask actually goes"*. This component is that ask,
answered where it is made instead of by a page. **The name is in the language already, which
is the test BRANDING sets for any new component.**

**Why `<details>` and not a JS panel.** Three reasons, all structural:
1. **It works with no JavaScript at all** — open, close, keyboard, screen reader, all
   native. The site's whole degradation posture is that a page is complete before any script
   runs; a contact route that needs JS is the worst possible place to break that.
2. **`<summary>` is natively focusable and operable on Enter and Space** and carries implicit
   `aria-expanded`. A `<button>` plus a panel needs all of that written, and this repo has
   already shipped one tab-order defect from a hand-built panel (BRANDING §5.10, the
   SECTIONS index).
3. **The grammar exists.** `.dx` disclosures are already the frozen component on `/about`
   (eight bios) and in `work-shell.mjs`. The Ask borrows their open/close mechanics and their
   rotating mustard arrow, so **no new mark and no new interaction are introduced** —
   BRANDING §7.4's ban on new iconography is untouched.

**Rejected: a modal.** BRANDING §7.11 forbids modals outright — it would be the site's only
one, on a page meant to read as an instrument.
**Rejected: an inline `<form>` posting to a third party.** See AD-27.20.
**Rejected: keeping the navigation and making `/act` shorter.** `/act` earns its length; it
is the page for a reader who has *not* decided. The defect is routing a decided reader
through an undecided reader's page.

## AD-27.17 · The four audiences — the copy, and the exact mailto templates.

**One address for all four: `vimlendu@swechha.in`.** It is the address the owner offered for
this purpose, it is already published in `data/about-people.json` as his institutional
address, and it satisfies `about-people.json`'s `email_policy` (*"Only @swechha.in addresses
are published"*), which `/act`'s gate 10 enforces. **`swechhaindia@gmail.com` is not used
for an Ask** — it is the footer's general address and it fails the policy; `/farm`'s use of
it is a separate defect (AD-27.38).

**The subject line is the routing, and it is also the only analytics a `mailto` can ever
produce.** Pattern, invariable:

> `{Audience} enquiry — {Page name}`

so the owner's inbox sorts itself with no configuration and every message says which page
produced it.

**`.ask-l` — the one sentence — is the SAME on all four**, and it deliberately promises a
*person*, not an interval:

> This goes to Vimlendu Jha, Swechha's Executive Director. One person reads it, and the
> subject line already says which programme you are writing about.

**No turnaround time is printed anywhere.** The client wants a short turnaround and this
delivers it structurally — one hop, one human, a pre-sorted subject, a pre-written body — but
"we reply within two working days" is a claim about behaviour that nothing in the record
supports, and an unmet SLA on a site built on verifiable claims is the one lie that would
cost more than it buys. **Naming the person is the honest form of "short turnaround".**

### The four bodies

Each is five short lines at most. The client's point — *"no-one wants to read more after they
have decided"* — applies to writing as well as reading: a blank compose window is friction,

**`Your deadline` is the media-specific field and it is the one that actually creates a short

**Encoding.** Build the href as
`'mailto:vimlendu@swechha.in?subject=' + encodeURIComponent(subject) + '&body=' + encodeURIComponent(body)`
at **build time**, then HTML-escape `&` to `&amp;` in the attribute. Newlines become `%0A`
through `encodeURIComponent`; do not hand-write them. Keep every encoded href **under 900
characters** — the longest template above is well inside it, and 900 leaves headroom under
the ~2,000-character practical ceiling in Outlook.

**Rejected: four addresses, one per audience.** Three of them do not exist, and inventing
`press@` or `partnerships@` would be a published address nobody monitors — worse than the
generic page this ruling is replacing.
**Rejected: a "copy address" button.** It is a control that exists only where
`navigator.clipboard` does, it would be the site's first clipboard interaction, and an email
address is selectable text. The `.ask-a` sentence does the job in words, which is this site's
habit (BRANDING §5.9 — the social row is words, not icons).

## AD-27.19 · The Ask UPGRADES an existing control. It never adds one, and it must not break `/act`.

**The decision.** At every placement, the Ask **replaces** the CTA that currently navigates
to `/act`. The item's existing `act.href` and `act.label` in `data/work/**/*.json` are
**left exactly as they are**; the item gains a new sibling key.

```json
"act": { "label": "Bring your school", "href": "/act#partner" },
"ask": { "audience": "school", "subject_page": "Bridge the Gap" }
```

**Why this shape, and it is the most important sentence in section C.** `/act`'s entire
architecture (AD-25 §3) is *derived*: `build-act-page.mjs` reads every `data/work/*/*.json`,

no way claims stops the build. **If lane 2 rewrites `act.href` to a `mailto:`, `/act` loses
its ask list and the build fails.** Adding a sibling key changes nothing `build-act-page.mjs`
reads, so the derivation survives untouched, the return path a reader followed from
`/act#partner` still lands on the page that made the ask, and **`/act#partner` becomes the
Ask's tertiary link** — the reader who wants the long version still gets it.

**The one consequence to schedule:** lane 2 edits `data/work/**`, which lane 1's
`build-act-page.mjs` reads. `/act` must therefore be rebuilt after lane 2 lands. See
AD-27.60.

## AD-27.20 · A real form lands later. It is `/api/enquiry`, not a Google Form, and it replaces ONE element.

**The decision.** The `mailto:` is what ships now. Its successor is a first-party form
posting to a new `POST /api/enquiry` route built on the machinery the repo already owns. When
it lands, **the only thing that changes is the contents of `.ask-p`** — the `<details>`, the
summary, the label, the placements and the tertiary link are all unchanged, and the `mailto:`
stays underneath as *"or just write to us"*.

**Why a first-party route is now the right answer, superseding AD-25 §7's Google Form
recommendation.** AD-25 recommended one Google Form because there was nothing else. There is
now:
- `lib/subscriptions.ts` exports a **generic** `send(to, subject, text)` over Resend's plain
  HTTPS API (no SDK), plus `normaliseEmail()`, `newToken()`/`hashToken()` and a Neon client.
- `app/api/ward/subscribe/route.ts` is a **working, reviewed precedent** for exactly this
  shape: validate, refuse honestly when unconfigured, never store what it cannot email.
- `RESEND_API_KEY` and `DATABASE_URL` are already documented in `.env.example` and already
  needed by the ward alert job.

So the cost of a real form is **one route file and one script**, against a Google Form's
costs: an off-brand page in somebody else's typography, a third-party host receiving school
and funder contact details, a URL nobody in this session can create, and a build gate that
has to police the URL's shape. **A form that looks nothing like the site, on a domain that is
not the site, is a worse arrival than a mailto.**

**The contract, so it can be built without another ruling:**
- `POST /api/enquiry` accepts `{ audience, page, name, email, organisation, message }`.
  `audience` must be one of the four; `page` must resolve against `designRoutePaths()`;
  `email` through `normaliseEmail()`.
- **Unconfigured answers honestly**: `503 { ok:false, state:'not_configured', missing:[…] }`
  and the panel says the enquiry could not be sent and shows the address — the same posture
  as `subscribe/route.ts`'s comment: *"Accepting an address it cannot store or email would be
  the one genuinely dishonest thing this page could do."*
- On success it sends **two** emails through `send()`: the enquiry to `vimlendu@swechha.in`
  with the same subject line the `mailto` would have used, and a receipt to the sender that
  **says what happens next in the same words the page did** — a person, not a ticket number.
- Rate-limit by IP in Neon, and drop anything with a filled honeypot field. No CAPTCHA — the
  site has no third-party script and is not starting with one.
- **It does not replace `/act`.** `/act` remains the page for a reader who has not decided,
  and its derived ask list is unchanged.
- **It does not touch Give.** G-1 stands: there is no payment destination, and a form does
  not take money.

**Until it exists, nothing on the page hints at it.** No "form coming soon", no disabled
input. The `/act` placeholder that said the volunteer sign-up "isn't connected yet" is the
defect AD-25 was written to remove.

## AD-27.21 · Degradation, stated case by case.

| condition | what happens |
|---|---|
| **No JavaScript** | Everything works. `<details>` is native, `mailto:` is native. This is the design's baseline, not its fallback |
| **Reduced motion** | The arrow does not rotate; the panel still opens |
| **No mail client configured** | The `mailto:` button appears to do nothing. `.ask-a` — always visible in the open panel, never hidden behind a hover or a click — names the address in plain text and says exactly why the button did nothing |
| **A webmail-only user (Gmail in a browser)** | Chrome and Firefox can register Gmail as the `mailto:` handler; where the user has, the button opens a Gmail compose window with the subject and body filled. Where they have not, `.ask-a` covers it |
| **Screen reader** | `<summary>` announces as a disclosure with its expanded state. The panel's first element is a `.lbl` heading, so the content is announced with a shape |
| **Print** | `details` prints collapsed in most engines. Accepted: nobody prints this page to get an address, and forcing `details[open]` in a print stylesheet would be the site's first print rule |
| **Search engine** | The panel's content is in the DOM whether open or closed, so the address and the ask are indexable. This is a reason to use `details` rather than JS injection |

## AD-27.22 · The Ask is gated, in every generator that emits one.

Four assertions, in `build-work-pages.mjs`, `build-farm-page.mjs`, `build-about-page.mjs`
and `build-act-page.mjs`:

1. **Every `.ask` has a non-empty `data-ask` naming one of the four audiences**, and its
   subject line starts with the matching word. A mismatched pair means the inbox sorts wrong,
   silently, forever.
2. **Every `mailto:` on the page resolves to `@swechha.in`** — the same check `/act`'s gate 10
   makes. It is the rule that stops a personal address being published by accident.
3. **No page carries more than two `.ask` elements**, and no two share a `data-ask`.
4. **No unexpanded `{{token}}`, `SUBJECT` or `BODY` literal reaches the HTML** — the exact
   failure mode `/act`'s gate 15 exists for, and a `mailto:` with the literal string `SUBJECT`
   in it looks like a working button.

## AD-27.25 · The narrow column: `#done` loses the split, and a `.p-row` inside a split column ALWAYS stacks.

**The diagnosis, exactly.** It is not a collapsing `minmax`, not a `ch` cap, not a
percentage width. It is **a content-sized grid track inside a 5-of-12 column**:

- `.w7-pj-reg{grid-column:1/span 5}` — 5 of 12 columns of 1,148px = **464.3px**.
- `.p-row{grid-template-columns:minmax(0,auto) minmax(0,1fr)}` — `minmax(0,auto)` resolves
  its max to **max-content**, and the label is a `.lbl`: uppercase, `letter-spacing:.15em`.
  "A HUNDRED TO A HUNDRED AND FIFTY SCHOOLS A YEAR" measures **371px**. It takes it. Less the
  43.2px column gap, the `1fr` prose track is left with **49.7px** — six characters a line.
- **The author already found and half-fixed this.** `work-shell.mjs:644–657` stacks the row
  below 640 and says why in its own comment: *"in this section column one holds a written
  heading… the prose is squeezed."* Below 900 the **outer** split also stacks, so the row
  gets the full 1,148px and is fine. **Nobody wrote a rule for ≥900, where the outer split
  un-stacks and the inner row does not.** The fix was written against the phone and never
  re-measured in the composed case.
- And the shell already contains the verdict: `bridge-the-gap.html:2409–2411`, the `.wk-solo`
  comment — *"A split with nothing in its second column is not a split — it is a 5-column
  text block, which is 41% of the measure and unreadable."* The band then ships prose **plus
  a second nested grid** into that same block on nine pages.

**The decision, two rules, both required.**

1. **`#done` does not use `.w7-pj-split` at all.** The figure rail and the prose rows run at
   full `.wrap`. In `build-work-pages.mjs:1583–1591`, `splitBand({flip:true, left:…,
   right:…})` is replaced by a plain full-measure band: rail, then `proseRows(…)`, then the
   holes, then the ledger `<details>`. This deletes the 562px empty gutter (45% of the wrap)
   and the 464px container in one move.
2. **Structural, so it cannot recur:** any `.p-row` inside a split column stacks at every
   width.
   ```css
   .w7-pj-reg .p-row,.w7-pj-lead .p-row{grid-template-columns:minmax(0,1fr);row-gap:6px}
   .w7-pj-reg .p-row .body,.w7-pj-lead .p-row .body{max-width:none}
   ```
   **Written structurally rather than as a breakpoint**, which is the pattern BRANDING §6.1
   praises for exactly this reason: a breakpoint rule is a guess about which widths compose,
   and this defect is *proof* that the guess was wrong. A split column is never wide enough
   for two tracks, at any viewport, so the rule is unconditional.

**Both are needed.** Rule 1 fixes `#done` on nine pages. Rule 2 fixes every other `.p-row`
that ever lands inside a split — including the journey pages' `#done` if a later pass puts a
photograph back in the second column.

**Verification.** Re-measure `.p-row .body` chars-per-line on all nine pages at 1440 and
1920. **The floor is 45 characters a line**; anything below it fails. Today's worst is 4.9.

## AD-27.46 · The governing principle: the site earns a superlative query, it never asserts a superlative.

**The decision.** No page title, meta description, heading, JSON-LD field or sentence
anywhere on this site says Swechha is the best, the top five, or the leading anything **in
Swechha's own voice**. Superlative queries are earned two ways only: **third-party
recognition, quoted and attributed**, and **being the most complete honest answer on the
page**.

**Why this is not squeamishness.** This site's entire design language is built on verifiable
claims — every reading against its published limit, every figure traced to a source, every
hole left showing. **A lie in a meta description would be the one thing that discredits all
of it**, and it would be the easiest thing on the site to check. It is also bad SEO: Google
does not rank "Delhi's best environment NGO" by finding a page that says so.

**What the site does instead, and it is stronger:** it publishes the third-party record it
actually has. From Vimlendu Jha's own bio on swechha.in, already scraped into
`data/about-people.json` and already rendered on `/about`: **India Today and Outlook named
him one of India's top 25 youth leaders in 2004**; the International Youth Foundation
profiled him as one of the world's top 25 in *Our Time is Now*; **CNN International selected
Swechha's founder as one of six changemakers worldwide for *Be the Change* in 2007**,
following the work for over a year. Plus the institutional record in SOURCE-FACTS: the
European Commission, UNDP, UNV, UNEP, The American Embassy, the British Council, SRTT, CNN
International, NDTV. **Those are other people's superlatives, attributed, with dates. They
are the honest form of "top five".**

## AD-27.6-A · The AQI flash. AD-27.6's premise was false, and the numeral is now a build artefact that nothing repaints.

**Supersedes AD-27.6 clauses 2, 3 and 4.** Clauses 1, 5, 6 and 7 stand.

**TARGET, as ruled (AD-27.6 clause 2).** *"On an hourly feed most page loads land inside the
same hour, so **for most readers the flash disappears entirely.** This one `if` removes the
majority of the reported defect and is the cheapest half of this ruling."*

**ACTUAL, as measured 23 August.** **Nine of ten cold loads repainted the numeral.**
Ten loads at 1280×800 on a throttled connection (1.6 Mbit/s, 150ms RTT), cache and cookies
cleared between each, a MutationObserver armed on the numeral before first paint: committed
**311**, live **306**, text changed on loads 1–5 and 7–10 at between 2,542ms and 4,632ms.
Load 6 was the only clean one and it was clean because the fetch never resolved.

**Why the premise was false, in one line.** `data-committed` is a build artefact refreshed
by a **daily** job (`.github/workflows/data-refresh.yml`, `cron: '40 1 * * *'`) while the
CPCB reading moves **hourly**. On 23 August the homepage was serving a **22 August 10:00**
observation. Two values a day apart are almost never the same number, so the clause-2
comparison almost never held and the 160ms cross-fade ran on nearly every load — which, as
the critic put it, *"arguably draws the eye more than the old teleport."*

**THE MECHANISM ADOPTED.** *The reading arrives once.*

1. **The numeral is written by the build and no code may repaint it.** The repaint path is
   deleted from `home.html` and from `build-situation-air.mjs` — not guarded, deleted, along
   with the `.is-swap` CSS, the `.readout` transition and the `.p2-refresh` line that cited
   the replaced value. **The flash cannot recur because the code that produced it does not
   exist**, which is a stronger guarantee than any timing could be.
2. **The committed reading is refreshed hourly.** New job,
   `.github/workflows/air-hourly.yml`, `cron: '35 * * * *'`. It re-reads CPCB, and **only
   when the rendered figure, band, station or observation hour has actually moved** rebuilds
   the pages and commits; a still hour costs nothing and produces no deploy. Air gets its own
   job rather than dragging thirty datasets hourly — `data-refresh.yml`'s reasoning for being
   daily is correct for everything except the one feed that advances every hour.
3. **The live fetch's only remaining job is to confirm the chip.** `/api/air` is still read on
   load, from the edge, and it may change exactly one thing: the state chip flips PERIODIC →
   LIVE **if and only if** CPCB is publishing the same number the page is already showing
   **and** that observation is inside two hours. That is D-21.5's *"LIVE describes the fetch;
   the age describes the observation"* with the third condition it always implied and never
   stated — the figure under the badge has to be the figure the fetch confirmed. If CPCB has
   moved on, the chip stays PERIODIC over a committed reading carrying its own stamp, and the
   console names which condition failed.
4. **The stamp carries its date.** `Observed 10:00 IST.` was only true on the day it was
   built; the page was printing it a day later. Both pages now print the full stamp in
   `/api/air`'s own format — *"Observed 03:00 IST, 23 August 2026."*

**PROOF, ten cold loads, same harness, same throttle, 23 August: 0 of 10 repainted the
numeral** — first measured against the day-old dataset (0/10, chip correctly PERIODIC on all
ten because CPCB disagreed), then against a dataset refreshed by the new job's own sequence
(0/10, chip correctly LIVE on all ten). `/now/air`, measured the same way: **0 of 10.**

**CLS, measured with a `layout-shift` PerformanceObserver at 375×812, 1280×800 and
1440×900.** The numeral's rect is byte-identical before and after: `[21, 272.06, 116.52,
73.41]` at 375, `[64.02, 340.31, 315.70, 198.91]` at 1280, `[146.50, 437.55, 319.47,
201.27]` at 1440. **Zero shift attributable to the reading, at every width.** The chip flip
did register one — 0.00133 at 375, 0.00027 at 1280, 0.00019 at 1440, attributed to `state
live` and nothing else, because "Periodic" measures 77.56px and "Live" 43.81px and
`.s-hero-stamp>.wrap` is `justify-content:flex-end`. A `min-width:6.75em` on the chip
reserves the wider word; re-measured, **those entries are gone and total CLS at 375 is
exactly 0**. The entries that remain at 1280 and 1440 (0.0287 and 0.0204, at t≈160–215ms,
attributed to `navlinks` and `s-hero-acct`) are the webfont swap and are pre-existing.

**ONE FACT, ONE STATE.** Measured on both pages in the same minute: numeral **307** and
**307**, chip **Live** and **Live**, stamp *"Observed 03:00 IST, 23 August 2026"* on both.
They did not agree before this pass: the critic recorded the homepage reading PERIODIC /
*"Observed earlier today."* while `/now/air` read LIVE, off the same fetch, in the same
second.

**THE OPTIONS REJECTED, AND WHY — the parent brief named four and none was pre-approved.**

- **A neutral placeholder until the live read resolves (AD-27.6's option ii).** Rejected
  again, and on the same grounds AD-27.6 gave plus one the brief asked for: **a reader with
  JavaScript off would never get a reading at all**, which is a hard constraint on this pass.
  It is also the worst available LCP — the largest box on the page arrives last — and on
  fetch failure it leaves a hole where the site's whole argument is.
- **Serving the homepage's reading from the server at request time.** The most attractive of
  the four and the one rejected with most reluctance. Three reasons, in order of weight.
  (i) `CLAUDE.md` states the architecture as an invariant: *"There is no database and no
  server-side rendering per request — every route is prerendered (`○`/`●` in the build
  output, never `ƒ`)."* Turning `/` and `/now/air` into request-time renders contradicts it,
  and that is the owner's call to reverse, not this pass's.
  (ii) It cannot be verified from here. It depends on Vercel's output file tracing making
  `public/_pages/v3/*.html` readable inside a function, and on CDN behaviour that does not
  exist on `localhost`. **A mechanism that works locally and fails silently in production is
  worse than the defect it replaces.**
  (iii) It would re-implement each generator's derivation at request time. `/now/air`'s
  upgrade alone rewrites the numeral, the band word, the limit line, the six-pip scale, two
  strip cells, the source line, Delhi's row in the national panel and that panel's re-sort
  and rank sentence. **Two implementations of "what the reading means" is the exact drift
  this repository keeps paying for** — the critic had just fixed one instance of it.
- **Hourly refresh alone, keeping the live upgrade.** Rejected as a *complete* answer and
  adopted as *half* of one. Arithmetic: the job publishes at about :40, so a reader landing
  between :00 and :40 can be one CPCB hour behind and the numeral would still repaint. That
  is roughly a third of loads, not zero. It is in the mechanism because it makes the frozen
  figure current, not because it removes the flash — deleting the repaint removes the flash.
- **Keeping the numeral fixed and moving the live value into the provenance line.** Rejected
  in the form of *printing the newer figure*. A page whose caption names a number different
  from the one in the largest type on it has two readings on one screen, and choosing which
  to believe is work the reader should not be given. The chip carries the same information
  honestly and in one word: PERIODIC means *this is the committed reading*, LIVE means *CPCB
  is publishing this exact number right now*.

**RESIDUAL, stated rather than rounded to zero.** The numeral cannot change on any load —
that is structural, not statistical, and it holds whether the fetch succeeds, fails, or is
never made. What remains variable is the **chip**, which resolves to LIVE or stays PERIODIC
depending on CPCB and on the age of the last successful hourly build. Two consequences worth
knowing: if `air-hourly.yml` stops running (a lapsed `DATA_GOV_IN_KEY`, a paused schedule)
the page keeps a reading that is correct, stamped and PERIODIC, and simply gets older; and
in the ~20 minutes after each CPCB publication, before the job's deploy lands, the page is
one hour behind and says so.

**Two defects closed on the way, both of which only became visible once nothing repainted.**

- **The ticker's air cell was the last hand-typed AQI on the site, and it was wrong.**
  `home.html:3496` shipped **387** while the hero shipped 311 and `/now/air` shipped 311.
  Nobody had seen it because D-24.1's script overwrote the cell on every load — so with
  JavaScript on the defect was invisible and with JavaScript off the homepage stated two
  different AQIs for one city on one screen. `build-hero.mjs` now writes the figure, the
  `is-over` breach class and the aria-label from the dataset, matched exactly once or the
  build refuses.
- **The air slide's prose clause and its multiplier disagreed.** `.s-hero-cut` read *"3.9
  times the limit"* beside a `.mult` of *"3.1×"* — two statements of one division on one
  screen — because the runtime script rewrote the clause on every load and the build never
  did. Both are now written from the same arithmetic. The station name had the same shape of
  problem and is fixed the same way: a city's AQI is its **worst** station, so the monitor
  moves with the reading (Anand Vihar → Jahangirpuri inside one hour on 23 August), and the
  homepage was left naming a different monitor from `/now/air`'s.

**And one bug introduced by this pass, found and gated.** `build-situation-air.mjs` emits
its page script from a **template literal**, so `\d{2}` written once is read by JavaScript
before the page sees it and `(d{2})` is emitted — valid JavaScript, and a regex that matches
nothing. The observation-stamp parser on `/now/air` therefore returned `null` on every load,
the console said *"the feed did not stamp its observation"*, and the chip could never reach

will write the file. This is the same failure the critic fixed on the homepage
(`Date.parse` on an IST wall clock), in the same function, arrived at by a different route —
which is the argument for the executable gate rather than a third careful reading.

## AD-27.3a · One mark per bar. `/work/journeys` marks only `Journeys`.

**AMENDS AD-27.3's table.** The row *"`/work/journeys` | **also** `Journeys` | `page` | href
equals URL; both words may mark, they are different claims"* is **withdrawn**. Standing on
`/work/journeys`, the bar marks `Journeys` with `aria-current="page"` and marks nothing else.
Every other row stands unchanged.

**Why, in the order of how much each costs.**

1. **The table already rules against itself.** Its next row refuses two marks on
   `/work/journeys/<slug>` because *"two `true`s on one bar is noise"*. The objection is
   **stronger** here, because `Work` and `Journeys` are adjacent words in the bar. Measured
   at 1280: the two rules would be **42px and 74px wide with a 28px gap**, and the capture
   reads as one broken dashed rule under "WORK JOURNEYS", not as two claims.
2. **The distinction is invisible to the reader it would be shown to.** The CSS is
   `.nav a.nl[aria-current]{border-bottom-color:var(--mustard)}` — keyed on the attribute's
   *presence*, not its value. `page` and `true` both paint `2px solid #E1A32B`. "They are
   different claims" is true of the markup and false of the page.
3. **AD-27.1 fixed the mark at one meaning** — *"this page belongs to this chapter"* — and
   retired the scroll-spy precisely for giving one mark two meanings. Two marks on one bar is
   that defect by another route.
4. **The table's own derivation rules never produce two marks.** `page` where the marked
   element's href equals the URL; `true` where the word is the correct chapter but its href
   is the parent; nothing where neither is true. Nothing in that grammar authorises a second
   simultaneous mark. The row is the anomaly; the rules are the ruling, and the rules win.
5. **Assistive technology.** `aria-current` identifies *the* current item in a set. Marking
   parent and child in one `nav` announces two current locations — the ambiguity the
   attribute exists to remove, and what `work-shell.mjs`'s own model has said since AD-17:
   *"`aria-current` is one location, most-specific-wins."*

**Rejected: implementing the table.** It would change `workHeader(sections, current, url)`
from a single `current` label to a set, for exactly one URL out of thirty-five; it would make
`/work/journeys` inconsistent with its own four children, so clicking from the index into a
journey visibly *loses* an underline; and it would spend the site's only navigation mark on a
distinction no reader can see.

**Enforcement is structural, not editorial:** `current` is a single string, so `workHeader`
can mark at most one word. Anyone implementing the withdrawn row must change that signature
first, which is where the reasoning is recorded.

**VERIFIED, 23 August, read out of the built HTML for all fifteen pages the work shell
emits:** exactly one marked nav word per page (twice each — the bar and the hidden Menu panel
carry the same markup), `page` on `/work` and `/work/journeys`, `true` on the other thirteen,
and no page marks the wordmark, the GIVE chip or the search control. The mark computes
`2px solid rgb(225,163,43)` = `--mustard #E1A32B` on the marked word and `rgba(0,0,0,0)` on
every other.

## AD-27.6-C · The ticker head's clock is cut. A single read time was never true of that strip.

**The defect, found in a browser on the finished homepage.** The head read **"Delhi, then
India. · Five in window · one record · Saturday, 22 August 2026, 07:00 IST"** while the air
cell inside the same strip read **307** — the 03:00 IST, **23 August** observation the hero
now carries. **The strip's own stamp was a day and four hours behind its own contents.**

**The cause.** `home.html` hard-coded `data-at="07:00"` on `.s-ticker-stamp`, and `at()`
resolves a bare clock time to *today*, or to *yesterday* when that hour has not arrived yet —
so before 07:00 IST the strip stamped itself the previous day. That rule was correct when the
strip was rebuilt once a day at a fixed hour. **AD-27.6-A rebuilds air hourly, and a fixed
clock cannot follow a moving reading.** This is the same class of defect as the one the pass
spent itself closing: one fact, two states on one screen.

**THE RULE ADOPTED: the head carries no read time at all, and says where the times are.**
`.s-ticker-when` now ships one static sentence — **"Each reading is dated on its own page."**

**Why, and it is AD-05 R4's own argument one step further.** R4 removed the LIVE dot from
this exact line because a page-level mark *"could assert more than the data supports"* over a
strip that mixes states, and it recorded that an honest aggregate is impossible here. **A
single read time is that same claim in another shape**, and it survived only because nobody
examined it. Six cells, six cadences: air hourly, Yamuna a monthly grab sample, burnt area a
season, forest loss a 2001–2025 cumulative, Climate Event a completed year, and the Impact
slot not a reading at all. **No hour is true of all six.**

**Rejected: stamping the head with the strip's freshest observation** — the air hour, written
by `build-hero.mjs` beside the air injection it already does. It keeps the stamp in step with
the cell, and it is what the obvious fix looks like, but it makes the claim **precisely wrong
instead of vaguely wrong**: it would assert an hourly freshness over a twenty-five-year
cumulative. It also puts a second copy of a date the hero already states correctly one screen
above, attached to the reading it actually describes — and two copies of one fact is how they
come to disagree.

**Degradation (D-01.11).** The old stamp shipped its date span **empty** so a JS-off reader
saw *"07:00 IST"* — less, never wrong. The replacement is static text: it is **identical with
JavaScript off**, and it needs no script at all. The head's date branch, `stamp()` and the
day/month-name tables it was the only caller of are **deleted**, not left guarded — with the
element gone the branch would have found nothing and looked like working code, which is the
silently-dead-IIFE pattern this file has been bitten by twice. `at()`, `reading()`, `warn()`,
`ago()` and `p2()` stay; the hero's two dated `<time class="s-hero-age">` readings still use
them.

**THE GATE, which is the part that matters.** `build-hero.mjs` now **refuses to write
`home.html`** if the ticker head regains a `data-at`, an `.s-ticker-stamp`, an
`.s-ticker-date`, a clock-shaped string or a month name. The stamp cannot drift from the air
cell again because it cannot come back without failing the build. HTML comments are stripped
before the test, deliberately: the note beside the head quotes the deleted attribute and the
wrong date it rendered, because that is the evidence for why it is gone, and a gate that
fired on its own explanation would be paid off by deleting the explanation. A second
assertion checks the replacement sentence is still true — **five of the six cells link to a
situation page that dates its reading**, the sixth being the record, exactly as the count
line beside it says.

**Negative-tested, because a gate nobody has seen fail is not a gate.** Restoring the
original `<span class="s-ticker-stamp" data-at="07:00">` makes `build-hero.mjs` print
*"ticker head: it carries a data-at attribute and the .s-ticker-stamp element and the
.s-ticker-date element and a clock time"*, refuse to write, and **exit 1**. Restored, it
passes.

**Measured before → after,** at 375×812, 1280×800 and 1440×900. Head height **46.03 / 31.25 /
31.25 — unchanged at all three**. Document height **10,245 / 10,068 / 10,906 — unchanged at
all three**. The slot itself widens 255.38 → 292.09px, which `space-between` absorbs by
moving the middle item 18.36px left at 1280 and 1440; three items, one line, still flush at
both ends, and at 375 the count is still `display:none` and the head still wraps to two lines
exactly as before. Read at 1280: the head now reads *"Delhi, then India. · Five in window ·
one record · Each reading is dated on its own page."* over an air cell of 307, matching the
hero.

**Not a defect, checked before it was written up.** The hero deck's counter was reported as
possibly reading "1 of 6" against four slides. The DOM has four slides, four tabs and one
counter reading **"1 of 4"** at 375 and 1440; the misreading came from a downscaled capture.
Recorded so it is not re-opened.
