# The copy standard — owner's brief, 23 August 2026

The owner commissioned a full copy relook and wrote the standard himself. It is
recorded here verbatim, because every editing pass from now on works to it and a
paraphrase would drift. Where this file and an older AD ruling disagree, **this
file wins** — it is the client speaking about his own voice.

Read with `2026-08-23-AD-28-provenance-strip.md`, which is the deletion rule this
brief authorises. That file says what comes out; this one says what must survive.

---

## Verbatim

> Please do a full copy relook across the Swechha website. This is a calibration
> and refinement exercise, not a wholesale rewrite.
>
> The website already has a strong voice. The copy is modern, youthful, sharp,
> confident, catchy and often hook-driven. Keep that character intact. Do not
> flatten the writing into generic NGO, corporate, institutional or AI-sounding
> language.
>
> **The core issue to fix.** In some parts of the website, the copy is becoming
> overly transparent about internal data methodology and provenance. It is
> explaining things the reader does not need to know, for example: how a number
> was calculated · why a number is unavailable or missing · why data has not been
> updated · who provided or verified a number internally · whether a number came
> from the Executive Director, owner, team member or another internal source ·
> the exact date on which an internal figure was supplied or confirmed ·
> unnecessary caveats about internal reporting methodology · explanations of why
> a particular metric cannot be displayed.
>
> This level of granularity is not required on the main Swechha pages and makes
> the copy feel defensive, cluttered and less confident.
>
> **Important exception: DO NOT disturb data transparency where it matters.** For
> the Situations and Environmental Intelligence sections: Keep all existing
> numbers intact. Keep all source citations and source links intact. Keep
> attribution, dates, data references and methodology-related context wherever it
> is currently necessary to establish credibility and accuracy. Do not simplify
> away important source information. Do not alter, round, reinterpret or remove
> data. Do not change the factual meaning of any statistic. Preserve the
> integrity of live-data and intelligence pages. These sections are specifically
> data-driven and should retain their evidence, sources and context.
>
> **For the rest of the website.** Outside Situations and Environmental
> Intelligence, apply a much stronger editorial filter. If a page says something
> useful and compelling, do not burden it with backstage information about where
> every internal number came from or how it was reported. The reader should see
> the story, impact, scale and relevance — not the internal audit trail.
>
> For example, prefer: *30 schools. Thousands of plants. Cleaner air, richer
> biodiversity and spaces where children learn directly from nature.* Over copy
> that explains exactly who supplied the school count, when it was confirmed,
> whether the number is self-reported, or why another impact metric is not
> available.
>
> That does not mean removing genuine facts. It means presenting facts cleanly
> and confidently, without unnecessary metadata.
>
> **What to preserve.** Please retain as much of the existing copy as possible,
> especially:
> 1. **The tone and personality** — modern, youthful, energetic, intelligent,
>    bold, human, occasionally playful or provocative where appropriate.
> 2. **The sharpness of the hooks** — do not weaken strong headlines. Do not
>    replace memorable lines with safe generic statements. Keep punchy openings
>    and strong calls to action.
> 3. **Program and project descriptions** — in most cases the existing
>    descriptions of programs, projects, campaigns and initiatives are already
>    good. Keep them substantially intact unless there is a clear problem.
> 4. **Team descriptions** — the existing writing about the team should generally
>    remain intact. Remove unnecessary internal provenance or reporting detail,
>    but do not sterilize the personalities or achievements of people.
> 5. **Existing structure and intent** — do not rewrite every section simply for
>    the sake of rewriting. If something is already working, leave it alone.
>
> **Editing principle: subtract before you rewrite.** Your first instinct should
> be to cut unnecessary explanation, not replace good writing with new writing.
> For every paragraph, ask: *Does the visitor need to know this to understand,
> trust or act on this page?* If the answer is no, remove or simplify it.
>
> In particular, remove or compress: repetitive explanations · unnecessary
> disclaimers · internal data provenance · internal verification notes ·
> reporting dates that have no public relevance · explanations for unavailable
> metrics · methodology details outside genuinely data-driven sections · repeated
> statements of impact · long setup sentences before getting to the point ·
> AI-style over-explanation.
>
> **Keep it crisp.** The overall website should feel clean, fast and easy to
> scan. Where possible: use shorter paragraphs · avoid saying the same thing
> twice · get to the point faster · retain strong one-line statements · let
> important facts breathe · avoid excessive qualification · do not turn every
> claim into a disclaimer. However, do not mechanically shorten everything. A
> good piece of descriptive or narrative copy can remain longer if it is doing
> meaningful work. The goal is not "shorter at all costs." The goal is: **Maximum
> clarity. Maximum impact. Minimum unnecessary explanation.**
>
> **Avoid these mistakes.** Do not: convert the site into bland corporate copy ·
> make it sound like a conventional NGO annual report · remove the youthful edge ·
> remove strong hooks merely because they are informal · replace distinctive
> writing with generic AI phrases · add new jargon · add unnecessary headings ·
> overuse words like "impact," "empower," "sustainable," "transformative," or
> "innovative" · add disclaimers where none are needed · expose internal data
> collection or reporting processes unless genuinely relevant · change numbers,
> facts or sources in the protected data-driven sections · rewrite good program,
> project or team copy unnecessarily.
>
> **Editorial test.** After reviewing each page, ask: 1. Is this sharp? 2. Is this
> clear on first reading? 3. Does it still sound like Swechha? 4. Is there
> unnecessary explanation behind a fact that the reader simply does not need?
> 5. Can anything be removed without losing meaning or personality? 6. Have I
> preserved the strongest existing lines? 7. Does this feel modern and youthful
> rather than bureaucratic?
>
> **Final objective.** The site should feel like a confident organisation that
> has nothing to prove through excessive explanation. Let the work, ideas,
> projects, stories and credible facts speak for themselves. Preserve the
> existing sharpness. Preserve the style. Preserve good copy. Cut the
> over-explanation. Keep the data-heavy Situations and Environmental Intelligence
> sections rigorous, sourced and intact. Everywhere else, remove unnecessary
> internal transparency and methodology details unless they genuinely add value
> to the visitor.
>
> Please review the copy page by page and make only those changes that genuinely
> improve clarity, brevity and impact.

## The two regimes, as a table an editor can work from

| | **Situations + Environmental Intelligence** — `/now`, `/now/air`, `/now/yamuna`, `/now/heatwave`, `/now/forest-fire`, `/now/forest-loss`, `/now/climate-event` | **Everywhere else** — `/`, `/about`, `/work/**`, `/farm`, `/impact`, `/act`, `/stories/**`, `/publications`, `/search` |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | Untouched. Never rounded, reinterpreted or removed | Kept, but presented clean |
| Source citations and links (CPCB, DPCC, FIRMS, GFW, IMD, WHO) | **Kept** — they are the substance | **Removed** — the figure stands on its own |
| Observation dates, station names, dataset names | **Kept** | Removed |
| Methodology notes | **Kept** where they stop a misreading (detections ≠ fires; attributable share ≠ death certificates; measured vs modelled) | Removed |
| Who supplied a figure internally, and when | Removed — this was never source citation, it was bookkeeping | Removed |
| `SOURCE-FACTS §NN` and other internal repo references | **Removed** — these point at our own working files, not at a source | Removed |
| "What this page cannot say yet", gap counters, empty-state confessions | Removed. A hole in *external data* may be stated as a fact about the data where that is the story; it is never an apology about our page | Removed |
| The page narrating its own construction | Removed | Removed |

## The one line to hold on to

**Subtract before you rewrite.** A deletion pass done carelessly leaves grey copy
behind, and grey copy is a worse outcome than the over-explanation it replaced.
When a citation or a caveat comes off the end of a sentence, stop there. Do not
write a replacement clause; do not reach for a heading; do not turn the gap into
a disclaimer. The strongest existing lines are the asset — the editing exists to
let them be heard.
