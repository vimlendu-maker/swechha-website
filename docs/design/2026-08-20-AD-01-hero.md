# AD-01 — THE HERO

Art direction review, 20 August 2026. Section 1 of 12. Nothing below the ticker seam is in scope.

**How this was measured.** Every number below came off the live page through the Chrome
DevTools Protocol with `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` — real device metrics, not a
window size — at 375×812, 375×635, 414×736, 768×1024, 1024×800, 1280×700, 1440×720,
1440×900, 1440×1500 and 1920×1080. Screenshots were taken through the same session, so
the captures and the geometry agree. New captures are in `docs/design/img/sections/`
(`ad-hero-1440x900.png`, `ad-hero-1440x720.png`, `ad-hero-1024.png`, `ad-hero-768.png`,
`ad-hero-375.png`, `ad-kiss-1440.png`, `ad-kiss-slide3.png`, `ad-tabmark.png`,
`ad-bar-1440.png`, `ad-slide2.png`, `ad-slide3.png`).

**Two corrections to the brief, before anything else.**

1. `docs/design/img/sections/hero-mobile.png` is not a valid capture. It was taken with
   `--window-size=375,1400` and no device emulation, so the page laid out wider than 375
   and the PNG was cropped. The clipped body copy, the bands running off the right edge
   and the truncated ticker date in that file **do not exist**. In a properly emulated
   375 viewport `document.documentElement.scrollWidth === 375` and every hero element's
   right edge lands at 355, inside the 20px gutter. Delete or re-take that file before
   anyone else reads it.
2. Four of the five "known wrong" items in the handoff have already been built out and
   are now correct: the rail no longer slices the numeral, the hero is no longer 1,029px,
   there is no 500px dead space left of the readout, and the rail does not evaporate at
   375. Verified visually and by measurement, at every width. See §1.

## 1. What the hero is doing right

Short list. All of it is load-bearing; changing anything below must not cost any of it.

**The rail contract is correct, and it is the best thing in the band.** `.rl::after` at
`left:100%` + `margin-left:.06em` on a `width:max-content` wrapper (home.html:268–271).
Measured: at 1440 the numeral box is 146.5→466, the rule sits at 482.3 with a 16.32px
kiss (= .06 × 272px); at 375 the box is 21→137.5 and the rule at 143.5 with a 5.95px kiss
(= .06 × 99.2px). Identical optical gap at both ends of a 2.7× scale range. Because the
rule starts at 100% of a shrink-wrapped box it cannot cross a digit — confirmed by eye in
`ad-kiss-1440.png` (4× zoom on the "2" of 412) and `ad-kiss-slide3.png` (8.7× on the "2"
of 512). **Open item 3 in the handoff is a yes.** The kiss is right at 1440, at 1024
(12.9px on a 215px numeral), at 768 and at 375. Sign it off.

**The breach contract holds.** State changes colour and weight and nothing else: the rule's
x is 482.3 on the breach slides *and* on the non-breach slides; the numeral does not move.
`.s-hero-acct` computes `border-left: 0px` — the old `.rail-r` border is gone.

**Clearance is measured from the rule, not from a column edge.** At 1440 the account column
begins at 520.3, exactly 32.0px after the rule's right edge (482.3 + 6). At ≤1023 that
ladder steps to 24px, at ≤860 to 16px. This is what makes the text collision structurally
impossible, and it is worth more than it looks.

**The band has one spine.** The h1 (146), the readout (146.5), the provenance plate (146.5)
and the control bar (146) share a left edge at every desktop width. Do not centre anything.

**Height, on desktop, is solved.** 825px at 1440×900; 891px at 1440×1500 and 1920×1080;
707px at 1440×720. Inside the 100vh / 720–900 spec at every window. The hero also lands a
consistent 12–13px short of the fold so the ticker's top hairline shows — a good reveal.
(One warning for whoever touches it: that 13px is not a stated intent, it is the residue of
`--s-hero-frame` budgeting 62px for a 63px nav and 92px for a 79px bar, home.html:720.
"Correcting" those two constants deletes the reveal. Leave them and write the intent down.)

**The Monsoon slide is the through-line stated out loud.** "Above normal is not the same as
illegal, so this page does not turn red for it" — and the page obeys itself: no `breach`
class, no red, four of six bands filled, verdict in off-white. That sentence plus its
rendering is the most credible thing in the band. Protect both.

**The reading sits on solid ground, not on a gradient.** Verdict, account, bands and
provenance are all on `--ground`, never on the photograph. Contrast is not being asked of
a scrim.

## 2. Defects — ranked, most damaging first

### BOTH WIDTHS

---

**D1 — There is no navigation at all below 768px.** *(mobile-only in effect; caused by a
desktop-scoped rule)*

**Where.** `ad-hero-375.png`, the header band, y 0–114 (device px) — wordmark left, GIVE
right, nothing else. Measured at 375×812: `getComputedStyle('.navlinks').display === "none"`
and `getComputedStyle('.navscroll').display === "none"`.

**Cause.** home.html:345 `.navscroll{display:none}`; home.html:348
`.navlinks{display:none!important}` inside `@media (max-width:940px)`; home.html:371
`@media (max-width:767px){.navscroll{display:none!important}}`. The comment at
home.html:366–370 says the trade is deliberate — "Every link the chip row carried is in
the footer."

**Cost.** The footer's link grid begins at y = 8,992 of a 9,507px document — 94.6% of the
way down. On a phone the reader cannot reach Work, Journeys, Impact, Farm or Record, and
cannot reach the Environmental Intelligence index at all (the "Now" link goes to
`intelligence.html`, a different page — there is no other route to it from the homepage
header). This is not a hero defect strictly speaking, but it is the first thing the hero
sits under and it is the most damaging thing on the band. The mobile doctrine asked for a
one-row 56px header and got one; it did not ask for the site to lose its navigation.

**Fix.** Restore `.navscroll` below 768 but make it **non-sticky** — let it scroll away
with the page while only the 56px bar sticks. Zero permanent chrome, no JS, no modal
pattern the site does not otherwise have, and the doctrine's actual objection (13% of the
viewport permanently, plus a row clipped mid-word with no affordance) is answered. Give it
the 8px right-edge mask the hero's own tab row already uses at home.html:851–853. See Q1.

**D2 — At every realistic viewport the photograph is almost entirely under a gradient, and
below a 790px window it is *entirely* under one.**

**Where.** Compare `ad-hero-1440x900.png` (India Gate legible) with `ad-hero-1440x720.png`
(same frame, same crop — a dark textured band; the monument is barely discernible). And
`ad-hero-375.png`, y 114–422 device px: 25px of clean picture between two veils.

**Cause.** Three independently-scaled layers stack on one frame.
`.s-hero-shot` height = `clamp(628px, 100svh − 154px, 812px)` (home.html:720, via
`--s-hero-frame`). `.s-hero-mast`'s gradient lid is 111.8px derived from `vw`
(home.html:723–727). `.s-hero-panel::before`'s dissolve is `clamp(88px,10vw,124px)` =
124px, also `vw` (home.html:721, 748–752). And the opaque `.s-hero-panel` covers a fixed
399.7px of the frame's foot.

**Measured.** Clean, unveiled photograph = shotH − 399.7 − 111.8 − 124 = **shotH − 635.5**.

| viewport | shot | clean picture | % of frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1440×1500 / 1920×1080 | 812 | 176.5px | 21.7% |
| 1440×900 | 746 | 110.5px | 14.8% |
| 1440×720 | 628 | **−7.5px** (the two veils overlap) | 0% |
| 1280×700 | 628 | −7.5px | 0% |
| 375×812 (own arithmetic: 154.3 − 61.5 mast − 67.5 fade) | 154.3 | **25.3px** | 16.4% |

The zero-crossing is at a **789px window height**. A 13" laptop in Chrome with a bookmarks
bar is about 1440×760. So on an extremely common machine the hero photograph is a gradient.

**Cost.** This is precisely the failure the code comment at home.html:705–709 says it
measured and fixed ("33px of unveiled picture … it read as a black rectangle"). It was
fixed at ≥900 and reintroduced below 790 and at phone size. The band's stated hook is "a
three-digit numeral … standing over a halftoned India Gate in the haze"; at 375 the reader
gets 25px of haze, and at 720 none. The organisation's one piece of photographic evidence
in the opening band is unreadable.

**Fix.** Derive both veils from the frame instead of from `vw`: mast lid ≤ 14% of `shotH`,
panel dissolve ≤ 16% of `shotH`, and assert a floor — at least 40% of `shotH` must be
unveiled at every viewport. On the 628px floor that yields 88 + 100 and leaves 251px clean.
At 375 (154px frame) it yields 22 + 25 and leaves 107px clean, which needs the h1 to stop
relying on an 80%-black lid (it has a `text-shadow` already, home.html:729). See Q2.

**D3 — The hero's own reading is stated twice, at the same optical level, 12px out of
register.** *(desktop-only)*

**Where.** `ad-hero-1440x900.png`, y 636–696, the strip immediately under the numeral and
under the why paragraph.

**Measured at 1440×900.** Four small elements interleave across 60px in two columns with no
shared baseline: `.bands` 636.4–648.4 → `.unit` ("AQI, 24-HOUR ROLLING") 648.6–667 →
`.limit` ("CPCB SAFE LIMIT 100. LIMIT BROKEN.") 660.4–678.8 → `.mult` ("4.1× the limit")
667–695.8. The `.unit` and `.limit` baselines are **11.8px apart**, both set in the same
11.5px Archivo micro-caps, in adjacent columns 54px apart.

**Cause.** `.s-hero-num-side` stacks under the numeral inside `.s-hero-read`
(home.html:756–759) while `.s-hero-scale` gets `padding-top:var(--gap-block)` = 57.6px
inside `.s-hero-acct` (home.html:764). The two columns are laid out from different origins
and happen to land 12px apart.

**Cost.** A 12px miss is worse than a 60px one: it reads as a mistake rather than as two
levels. And the two lines say the same thing — "4.1× the limit" and "limit 100, broken" are
one fact in two voices, arriving as a matched pair of footnotes. The band's strongest
evidence loses half its force to a near-miss.

**Fix.** Give them different levels, not different columns. Either put the multiplier on the
`.limit` baseline exactly (one shared value, not two independent stacks), or lift
`.s-hero-scale` so the bands sit level with the why paragraph's last line and the limit line
clears the multiplier by a full `--gap-block`. Do not solve it by deleting either line —
both are approved copy and both are evidence.

**D4 — The deck's active-tab marker never paints. It is clipped by its own container.**

**Where.** `ad-tabmark.png` (16× zoom on x 144–264, y 816–858 at 1440). Nothing above "AIR".
`ad-bar-1440.png` at 3.7× confirms it at working scale.

**Measured.** `.rig-tabs button[aria-selected=true][data-breach=true]` computes
`border-top: 3px solid rgb(241,72,78)` — the rule is live. But `.rig-tabs` is
`overflow-x:auto` (home.html:397), which forces `overflow-y` to `auto`, making it a 15px-tall
scroll container; each button carries `margin-top:-14px` (home.html:400) and so overflows
14px above the container's padding box. `tabsTop 836.5`, `btnTop 822.5`, **clippedAbove
14.0px**. The 3px marker sits at 822.5–825.5 and is discarded. Same at 375 (button top
758.2, container top 772.2).

**Cost.** The only thing distinguishing the live situation from the other three is a colour
step from `#9C9585` to `#FBF8F0` in 11.5px caps — and the "1 of 4" counter that states it
sits 726px away (see D5). The deck reads as four labels, not as a selector.

**Second, worse consequence if a coder "fixes" only the clipping.** The marker is **red on a
control**. The closed list says red is "a published legal limit broken … never on a control".
Un-clipping it as written puts red on a `<button>`.

**Fix, decided.** Remove the clip (`.rig-tabs{overflow-x:auto;overflow-y:visible}` will not
work — a scroll container clips both axes; give the buttons `padding-top` inside the box and
drop the negative margin, or move the marker to a `::before` on the button's text baseline),
**and delete the red variant at home.html:408–409.** The selected marker becomes `--fg`
off-white. Not mustard: the tab row sits 90px above a ticker that must never carry mustard,
and the band's single mustard act ("The full instrument") should stay single. `.rig-tabs`
is shared with the intelligence page and the journeys rail — fix it once, check all three.

**D5 — The deck's index and the deck's controls sit 726px apart with nothing between them.**
*(desktop-only)*

**Where.** `ad-hero-1440x900.png`, y 809–888.

**Measured.** Last tab's ink ends at x = 433.3; "1 of 4" begins at x = 1159.5. Void = 726.2px
at 1440, 726.2px at 1920 (the bar is capped at 1148 so the void is constant), 731.6px at
1280. The counter and the arrows are 12px apart, i.e. already one object; the counter aligns
with nothing above or below it (the mustard link above starts at 1101.8).

**Cause.** `.rig-tabs{flex:1}` (home.html:397) makes the tab strip absorb all slack, and
`.count` + `.rig-arrows` are pushed to the right edge as a pair.

**Cost.** The four tabs and the "1 of 4" state the same fact — which of four you are looking
at — and the reader has to cross the page to connect them. It also makes the tab row read as
a second navigation rather than as this deck's index (it is 11.5px Archivo caps at .13em
tracking, 750px below a nav in 12px Archivo caps at .13em tracking).

**Fix.** Keep the arrows flush right — they sit on the page's right rule directly under
"THE FULL INSTRUMENT →" (both end at 1294.5), which is a real column. Move `1 of 4` to sit
immediately after the last tab at the band's own clear distance (`--s-hero-clear`, 32px at
≥1024). The bar then reads as [index · count] … [transport], two objects instead of three
orphans. One line: `.s-hero-bar .rig-tabs{flex:0 1 auto}` +
`.s-hero-bar .count{margin-right:auto;margin-left:var(--s-hero-clear)}`.

**D6 — The account column jumps 59.2px sideways when the reader changes slide.**

**Where.** Compare `ad-hero-1440x900.png` (slide 1) with `ad-slide2.png` (slide 2).

**Measured at 1440.** `.s-hero-acct` left edge: 520.3 on Air (412), **461.1 on Yamuna (0.0)**,
520.3 on Monsoon (512), 520.3 on Forest fire (118). Column width 774.2 → 833.4 → 774.2.
Because the readout is `width:max-content` (home.html:757) and the rule is anchored to it,
a value containing a decimal point is 59.2px narrower than three tabular digits, so the
verdict, the why paragraph, the six-band scale and the limit line all shift left and re-wrap
as the reader arrows across a deck that switches *in place*.

**Cost.** Small per slide, but the deck's whole premise is that only the reading changes.
The frame lurching is the one thing that tells the reader this is a slideshow rather than an
instrument.

**Fix, decided.** Make the decimal point occupy a digit slot so all four values are exactly
three tabular characters: wrap it, `.readout .dp{display:inline-block;width:1ch;
text-align:center}`. The rule still kisses the last digit, the readout column is constant,
the account column is constant, and no positioning input is reintroduced. (The alternative —
pinning the account to a fixed grid line — breaks the "clearance measured from the rule"
contract that D-nothing above depends on. Do not take it.)

**D7 — The resting rail is not a mark.** *(both, worse on desktop)*

**Where.** `ad-slide3.png` at 1440 — the rule beside "512" is a hairline you have to look
for. `ad-kiss-slide3.png` at 8.7× shows it is really there.

**Measured.** Non-breach: `border-left: 1px solid rgba(251,248,240,.20)` (home.html:270). On
`#0D0D0B` that composites to about `#3D3C39` — **1.74:1** against the ground. One pixel, 200px
tall, beside a 320px-wide numeral. Two of the deck's four slides (Monsoon, Forest fire) are
non-breach, so the site's structural signature is effectively absent on half the hero.

**Cost.** The rail is the mark that carries into the timeline, the impact figures, the farm
ledger and the ticker. If it only exists when something is broken, it is a breach indicator,
not a rail — and the deck's most important editorial sentence ("this page does not turn red
for it") lands on a slide with no visible instrument at all.

**Fix.** Give the resting rule a weight derived from the numeral, exactly as the kiss is:
`--rl-w: max(1px, .012em)` → 3.3px at 272px, 2.6px at 215px, 1.2px at 99px, and hold the
colour at `--fg-3` (6.4:1) rather than `--hair`. Breach still steps to 6px + red, still grows
rightward, still never moves the numeral. The contract ("state changes colour and width
only") is untouched; only the resting value changes from a constant to a ratio.

**D8 — The hero's explanatory paragraph is the smallest running text in the band, at a size
and a measure that exist nowhere else in the system.**

**Where.** `ad-hero-1440x900.png`, "Four times the limit the Central Pollution Control
Board sets for a safe day…", y 529–579.

**Measured.** `.s-hero-why{font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.5;max-width:58ch}`

**Fix, decided.** `.s-hero-why{font-size:var(--t-body);max-width:62ch}` — the existing
`.body` ceiling — and delete the 16px override at ≤860. At 1440×900 the hero has 75px of
headroom under the 900 cap; this costs about 12.

**D9 — "YEAR ROUND" and "IN WINDOW" are drawn in the page's own vocabulary for a *closed*
window.**

**Where.** `ad-hero-1440x900.png` x 676–776, y 754–783 ("YEAR ROUND", dashed box);
`ad-slide3.png` ("IN WINDOW", same treatment).

**Cause.** `.tag-season{border-style:dashed}` (home.html:533). Dashed is already spoken for:
`.closed .rl::after{border-left-style:dashed}` (home.html:274) and
`.state.closed i{border-style:dashed}` (home.html:~301) both use dashed to mean *a window
that is shut*. Here it frames the two tags that mean the window is open.

**Cost.** Two things: the page contradicts its own state grammar in the band that defines it,
and a 1px dashed rectangle around 11.5px caps sitting between a source line and a live link
reads as a disabled button. It is not clickable.

**Fix, decided.** Solid hairline, or no box at all — set the validity window as plain
`--fg-3` micro-caps and reserve dashed for closed windows. Applies wherever `.tag-season`
appears.

**D13 — The halftone does not scale with the frame, so on a phone the photograph is texture.**

**Where.** `ad-hero-375.png`, y 114–422 device px: the India Gate resolves as an arch-shaped
absence and nothing else — no lamps, no trees, no plinth.

**Measured.** `.s-hero-shot::after` is a fixed 6px dot grid, stepping to 4px below 700px
(home.html:740–744). The frame goes from 746px tall at 1440×900 to 154.3px at 375 — a 4.8×
reduction — against a 1.5× reduction in pitch. Dot rows across the frame: **124 at 1440,
38 at 375**. The reproduction is being done at less than a third of the resolution for the
same subject.

**Ruling (the coordinator asked for one).** The pitch must scale with the frame, not with a
breakpoint. A halftone is a reproduction metaphor; its dots are read against the subject, not
against the screen. Holding a constant pitch means holding a constant *screen ruling* on a
subject that has shrunk 4.8× — which is why the monument dissolves. Coming off below a width
would be worse: the hero would be the one halftoned band that is not halftoned on the
platform most people see it on, and the treatment is on the closed list.

**Fix.** Derive it: `background-size: max(2px, calc(var(--shot-h) / 124))` with `--shot-h`
set from the same clamp that sizes the frame, and delete the `@media (max-width:700px)`
override at home.html:743–744. That gives 6px at 1440 and 2px at 375 — on a 2–3× phone
screen a 2px CSS dot is still 4–6 device pixels, so it still reads as a screen. **And
re-crop the mobile frame**: at 154×374 the monument occupies about 30% of the width with
empty plaza either side. 38 dot rows can describe a monument that fills the frame; they
cannot describe a plaza. Adjust `--zh` / `--op` on the `<img>` at home.html:1688 for the
≤700 case only.

**D14 — The mobile rail is better than the doctrine that describes it. Amend the doctrine,
not the code.** *(ruling, not a defect)*

The doctrine says the account column "drops below and is indented to the rule's exact
x-position". The build instead keeps the unit and the multiplier beside the rule and drops
the account block full-width beneath a hairline that is itself tinted red on a breach
(home.html:830–831).

**The build wins, and the numbers say why.** Indenting the account to the rule's x puts its
left edge at 143.5 on a 375 screen, leaving a 211px measure. At 16px Newsreader that is about
26 characters a line: the Yamuna slide's why paragraph would run 8–9 lines instead of 4,
adding roughly 110px to a band already 233px over budget, and the six-band scale would become
six 32px cells. The built version puts the vertical rule exactly where it means something —
the numeral on one side, its own account on the other — at a scale where an indent cannot be
afforded, and then hands off with a full-width hairline that carries the breach state. Same
mark, correctly re-shaped. Amend `mobileDoctrine` to describe what was built.

(D12 is the one correction it still needs: what hangs off the rule should be the identity,
then the unit, then the multiplier.)

## 3. Questions for the client

**Q1 — How does a phone reader reach the rest of the site?** (D1)
- (a) Restore the six-link chip row below 768, **non-sticky** — it is there on arrival and
  scrolls away; 56px of permanent chrome, 37px of one-time chrome, no JS.
- (b) Keep the 56px bar and add a 44px menu control opening a full-screen sheet; 0px extra
  permanent chrome, one tap, but it introduces a modal pattern the site does not have
  anywhere else.
- (c) Leave it: footer only.
**I would take (a).** The doctrine's objection was to a row that was permanently sticky,
clipped mid-word and had no affordance — not to the row existing. Making it non-sticky
answers all three complaints and costs nothing at rest. (b) is the fashionable answer and it
buries the site's own index behind an icon on the one page that is supposed to be an
instrument, not an app.

**Q2 — Is the hook still "the numeral standing over the India Gate"?** (D2)
- (a) No — the picture is a masthead band and the reading sits on solid ground beneath it.
  Then the fix is just arithmetic: derive both veils from the frame, hold a 40%-unveiled
  floor, and stop paying 400px of frame height for picture that is covered by an opaque
  panel.
- (b) Yes — then the panel's opaque ground comes off, the reading moves onto the photograph
  on `.duo-dim` (the bounded ramp is defined at home.html:160 and is used **zero times** on
  this page, and it exists for exactly this), and the halftone hero delivers what the
  art-direction line describes.
**I would take (a).** The current separation is why the reading holds up at arm's length in
daylight, and (b) would put a 272px numeral and a 58ch paragraph on a photograph at the top
of a page whose whole argument is legibility. But (a) means editing the `hook` line in
`2026-08-20-art-direction.json` so the words and the page agree — the through-line does not
allow the spec to describe something the page is not doing.

**Q3 — Is the hero's deck "today's worst broken limit", or "today's readings"?** (D7)
The section's `job` says the first. The build always opens on Air — `mark(0)` in
home.html:2308 with Air hard-coded first — and Air is not the worst reading in the deck:
412 against a limit of 100 is 4.1×; Yamuna's dissolved oxygen is 0.0 against a minimum of
5.0, which is not a multiple, it is an absence.
- (a) "Worst first", computed. The deck sorts by breach severity on load. This needs the
  stored `limit` that is already open item 5 in the handoff, and it means the opening
  photograph varies day to day.
- (b) "Today's readings", fixed order, Air first because air is what Delhi wakes up to.
**I would take (b)** and change the `job` line, because (a) makes the page's first image
unpredictable and the h1's relationship to it uncontrollable — and because the deck's real
argument is the Monsoon slide, which only works if non-breaches are allowed in.

**Q4 — May the 375 hero lose one sentence so it fits one visible screen?** (D10)
The band is 806–811px against ~635px of visible iOS Safari. D11 gives back ~98px for free.
The remaining ~75px has to come off the *tallest* slide, which is Yamuna's why paragraph
("The river enters Delhi alive and leaves it without oxygen. Twenty-two kilometres of it are
inside the city, under two per cent of its length, and they carry most of its pollution
load.") — about 7 lines at 375.
- (a) Cut the second sentence at ≤560 only. ~96px off every slide. Hero → ~617px + 57 nav.
- (b) Keep all copy and accept that the deck's controls are below the fold on a phone.
- (c) Keep all copy and move the controls above the provenance plate instead.
**I would take (a).** The doctrine's own rule is "cutting copy is not the mobile strategy —
changing shape is", and this is the one place where no change of shape is left: the sentence
is a single measure of running text. The clause that survives is the one that names the
river; the clause that goes is the arithmetic, which is on the situation page. But it is
approved copy and the cut needs a yes.

**Q5 — The tab row and the ticker.** The hero's four tabs sit 90px above the ticker's seven
cell labels; both are 11.5px Archivo caps in `--fg-3`, and four of the seven ticker labels
*are* the four tab labels. One switches a panel in place, the other leaves the page. The
hero's red 412 also repeats as the ticker's first red value 254px below it. This is already
open as question 2 in `2026-08-19-section-audit-salvage.json` (Ticker) and the answer
changes work in both bands, so it should be answered once, now, not twice:
- (a) The ticker is the hero deck's index — mark the active situation in the strip and let
  the first four cells drive the deck in place.
- (b) They are independent — then the ticker's labels must drop to 10.5px and the two rows
  need more than 90px between them.
**I would take (b).** Making the ticker drive the hero gives one strip two jobs and breaks
its declared status as chrome; the cheaper and truer move is to stop them looking alike.

## 4. What I would NOT change

**The rail.** Do not restore `.rail-r`'s border. Do not reintroduce `--ovh`, `--lw` as a
positioning input, or any negative margin. Do not touch `--kiss`. D6 and D7 both stay inside
the contract on purpose — one pads a glyph, the other turns a constant into a ratio; neither
moves the numeral or re-parents the rule.

**The readout size.** 272px against 18px body is already about 15:1. Every problem in this
band is a framing problem; none of them gets better by making the number bigger. Same at 375
— the 99.2px floor is right, do not go under it and do not go over it.

**The separation of type from photograph.** Unless Q2 comes back (b), leave the reading,
the account and the provenance on solid ground. It is the reason the hero survives a phone
screen outdoors, and it is why the halftone can be as coarse as it is.

**The h1 at 67px.** It is 5vw-clamped rather than `--t-d1` (104px), so the masthead is
deliberately quieter than the statement band's display and much quieter than the readout.
That is correct: the reading is the display here, and the h1 is a masthead. Do not promote it
to `--t-d1` for consistency's sake.

**The single mustard act.** One mustard link ("The full instrument") in the band, plus GIVE in
the nav. The obvious instinct after D4 is to put mustard on the tab hover and the selected
tab. Resist it — the tab row sits 90px above a ticker that is forbidden mustard, and the
whole grammar rests on mustard being scarce.

**The 12–13px of ticker showing under the hero.** It is the scroll cue. Do not add a chevron,
a "scroll" label, or a bounce. And see the warning in §1 about the two constants that produce
it.

**The four-photograph deck.** The closed list says "halftone on exactly three full-bleed
heroes" and the hero is one of the three — but it contains four halftoned photographs, so the
page carries six halftoned images in total. That is within the letter of the rule (it counts
bands) and I would leave it. Two of the four (kans grass, grasses at dusk) are texture rather
than pictures, which matters less once the crop and the pitch are fixed (D13) and would
matter a great deal if Q2 came back (b).

**The ticker's own defects.** Twelve of them are already written up in
`2026-08-19-section-audit-salvage.json` and none has been re-verified here. That is section 2
of this pass, not section 1.
