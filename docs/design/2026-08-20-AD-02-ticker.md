- **#5.** At 375 the cells are 132px with `scroll-snap-align:start`. The viewport edge at

- **#10.** `grid-template-columns:repeat(7,minmax(0,1fr))` (home.html:1066).
- **#11.** The divider moved from `border-right` to `border-left` with a `:first-child`

And the two overtaken ones:

- **#7's prescribed fix is now illegal.** It asked for `color:var(--mustard)` on hover and

- **#6's hue half is closed.** `groundRhythm` grants the ticker the exemption by name. Do not
  re-open the red/green adjacency. The exemption covers **hue**; it does not cover
  **provenance**, and that half of the defect survives (D7).

### 0.2 AD-01's Q5 numbers are wrong, in the direction that made it look safer

AD-01 says the two rows are "~90px" apart; the salvage file said 93px. Measured ink-bottom
to ink-top: **80.3px at 1440 and 1920, 77.9 at 1200, 74.7 at 1024, 72.5 at 900, and 68.3px
at 768.** The gap *shrinks* as the viewport narrows. Nobody looked at the tablet case, and
it is the worst one. Full argument in D3.

AD-01 also says "both 11.5px Archivo caps in `--fg-3`". That understates it. They are
identical on six axes and differ on one: see the table in D3.

### 0.3 T4 is arithmetically impossible for this section, and the build was right to refuse it

`spaceRules` assigns T4 STRIP = `clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px)` top and bottom. At 1440 that is
43.2 × 2 = **86.4px of padding alone**, on top of a ~66px content stack — about 152px. The
same document's `perSection[1].height` says "strip — 96–120px. Currently 119px; this is the
one section already the right size. **Do not grow it.**" The two clauses cannot both hold.

The build ruled in favour of the height and wrote the ruling into the file
(home.html:1040–1044). That ruling is correct and should be promoted into
`2026-08-20-art-direction.json` so the next person does not re-litigate it: **the ticker's
vertical padding is chrome's own, like the nav's, and T4 does not apply to it.**

Measured heights: 113.3px at 1920, 112.8 at 1440 and 1440×720, 109.3 at 1024 / 768,
103.3 at 560, 119.5 at 414 and 375. All inside spec. The one exception is 320 (D12).

### 0.4 The ticker's 100px left offset from the hero is not a defect — do not "fix" it

At 1440 the ticker's date and first cell sit at x=46 while the h1, the readout, the
provenance plate, the control bar and every section head below sit at x=146. At 1920 it is
216 against 386. Below ~1290 the two converge (40.8 at 1200, 34.8 at 1024, 26.1 at 768,
20.0 at 375) because the gutter binds before either cap does.

That is the direction working, not a misregistration. `spaceRules` puts the ticker on
`.wide` (1580) and separately tells the hero to place its rail group "on columns 2–11" —
one grid column in from `.wide`'s edge. Both bands are where they were told to be. The
100px is a free gift to D3 and I would keep it even if it were an accident.

### 0.5 There are three different counts of the same set on one screen already

The client ruling arrives into an existing contradiction, not a clean slate.
`intelligence.html` defines **nine** situations (`h-air, h-yamuna, h-monsoon, h-fire,
h-forestloss, h-heat, h-stp, h-noise, h-waste`) and its own index copy reads
*"6 In window today / Of nine defined. Heat closed on 15 July."* Its rig renders **six**
panels. The homepage ticker shows **seven**. The homepage hero deck shows **four**. Nothing
on the homepage says nine exist. This matters for Q2.

## 1. What the ticker is doing right

Short. All of it is load-bearing and none of it may be spent paying for a fix below.

**The rail is correctly laid flat, and it is the best idea in the strip.**
`.s-ticker-v` is `width:max-content` and its `::after` is `left:0;right:0` on that box
(home.html:1078, 1084), so the rule is *physically incapable* of being wider or narrower
than the digits it reports. Seven readings, seven rule lengths — 34.7px under "412",
63.7 under "512mm", 51.3 under "14/18". That variation is the strip's texture and it is
earned by data. Do not equalise it and do not run it to the cell edge.

**The breach contract is intact and correctly scaled down.** State changes colour and
weight and nothing else: 1px `rgba(251,248,240,.38)` at rest, 3px `--red` on `is-over`,
growing *downward, away from* the numeral, so the numeral never moves. Green changes colour
only and stays at 1px, which is right — green is not a breach, and only a breach swells.

**Nothing is clipped anywhere, at any width, and it is structural rather than lucky.**
`.s-ticker-head-in` is `flex-wrap:wrap` (home.html:1055) instead of switching at a magic
width, and every `.s-ticker-name` carries `text-overflow:ellipsis` as a backstop that never
fires. The salvage file's clipped "512mm" and clipped date cannot recur by construction.

**The seven columns are one pitch.** `repeat(7,minmax(0,1fr))` — 212.56px at 1920,
192.6 at 1440, 136.3 at 1024. The dividers land on a rhythm, which is the difference between
an index and a table that failed to line up.

**The value scale is disciplined.** 24.48px at 1440 against a 272px hero readout — a ratio
of 11:1 — and 21px at 375 against 99.2px. The strip is chrome and it is sized like chrome.
Every instinct to make these numbers bigger is wrong.

**The `--fg-2` step on the timestamp is a real decision.** 10.85:1 against the labels' 6.14:1
separates a stamp from a column head. It is applied to the wrong one of the two head fields
(D4), but the mechanism is right and the fix is to extend it, not to undo it.

### D1 — At 375 the strip delivers 2.7 of its 7 readings, and the one affordance the direction licensed to prevent that renders **zero pixels**. *(mobile)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-375-truesize.png` (1:1) and `ad2-tk-375.png` (3×) — the strip ends after
"MONSOON / 512mm" with clean ground to the edge and nothing after it.
`ad2-tk-375-edge.png` is an 8× crop of x 255–375 covering the entire fade zone.

**Measured at 375×812.** `.s-ticker-rail` `scrollWidth` 964 against `clientWidth` 375 — 61%
of the strip is off-screen. Cells: 20→152, 152→284, 284→416. The edge cuts 91px into cell 3.
Cell 3's rightmost ink is "MONSOON" ending at **x = 364.8**, so the last 10.2px before the
edge is empty ground. The fade is `.s-ticker-frame::after`, `width:8px`,
`linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(21,21,18,0), var(--ground-2))` (home.html:1101–1104) — it
occupies **x 367→375, entirely inside that empty ground**. A gradient running from
transparent-ground to opaque-ground, painted over ground, with no ink beneath it, is a no-op.
Confirmed at 8×: the fade zone is a uniform field, indistinguishable from the rest of the band.

**And it cannot be repaired by widening it,** which is the finding underneath the finding.
Ink occupies roughly the left 60% of each cell, so whether the cut lands on ink is an
accident of viewport width: at 375 the edge falls 91/132 into cell 3, at 414 it falls
**130/132** — two pixels short of a whole cell, with 49px of empty ground behind the last
ink. There is no fade width that bites at both. A right-edge fade only states overflow when
the cut happens to land on a glyph, and across real phone widths it usually does not.

**Cost.** The ticker's declared job is to prove the instrument runs — *"seven live readings
across every situation"* — and its exemption from the red/green rule was granted **because
"it summarises every situation at once."** On a phone it summarises 38% of them, shows only
breaches (412 red, 0.0 red, 512mm neutral — the green never appears), and gives the reader
no reason to suspect otherwise. The exemption is being claimed on a promise the mobile build
does not keep.

**Fix, decided — the count, not the fade.** The strip is an index, and an index that scrolls
must say how many. Put the count in the head's right-hand field, in the page's own existing
"n of m" vocabulary (the hero deck's "1 of 4", the intelligence rig's "1 of 6", the
instrument's own "14 of 18"). It is correct at every viewport width, it costs no height
(the head is being cut to one line anyway — D10), and it is a computed readout rather than
new voice. Keep the 8px fade as a secondary cue and raise it to 40px at ≤560 where it can
sometimes bite; do not rely on it. Amend `mobileDoctrine`'s "hard 8px ground-coloured fade"
line to say the fade is a floor and the **count** is the affordance — the same way AD-01's
D14 amended the doctrine to describe what was actually built.

### D2 — The strip's only green figure — the one reading Swechha owns — is a dead link. *(both)*

**Where.** `ad2-intel-hwaste-1440.png`: navigating to the 7th cell's href lands on the
**Air** panel at the top of the page with no visible response. Compare
`ad2-intel-hstp-1440.png`, where the 6th cell's href works correctly.

**Measured.** `home.html:2009` points at `/design/v3/intelligence.html#h-waste`. On that
page `#h-waste` exists in the DOM but computes `display:none`, rect 0×0, `offsetTop` 0. The
rig renders six tabs (Air, Yamuna, Monsoon, Forest fire, Forest loss, Treatment) and the
pager reads "1 of 6". After navigation: `scrollY 0`, `.rig-track` `scrollLeft 0`, counter
"1 of 6", visible panel `h-air`, Air tab `aria-selected="true"`. `intelligence.html`
contains no reference to `location.hash` or `hashchange` at all.

The other six now work — `#h-stp` lands at `scrollY 707`, `scrollLeft 7200`, counter
"6 of 6", Treatment selected. So this is the **only** broken link of the seven, and it is on
the only green value on the page above the impact band.

**Cost.** An organisation whose whole argument is "every section is a record with its source
attached" puts its one recovered quantity — 6,890 tonnes pulled out of the river since 2000
— behind a link that does nothing. It is also the single cell a reader is most likely to
click, because it is the only good news in the row.

**Fix.** Add `waste` to the intelligence rig's visible set so `#h-waste` is a real panel
(the intelligence page's own copy already says it belongs there: *"it is the one place
inside the instrument where green is allowed"*). Then re-check all seven hrefs resolve to a
visible panel, and add the hash handler that page still lacks so a direct hit sets the track
position, the pager and the tab together rather than relying on the browser's anchor scroll.

### D3 — The hero's deck tabs and the ticker's labels are the same object, drawn twice, 68px apart. *(desktop and tablet, 768–1920; not a phone problem)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-768-seam.png` is the case that decides it: `AIR YAMUNA MONSOON FOREST
FIRE` appears twice, on the same left edge, in the same type, 68px apart, once with a
selection state and once with numbers under it. Also `ad2-tk-1440.png` (top band) and
`ad2-tk-1920-full.png`.

**Measured.** They are identical on six axes and differ on one:

| | hero tab | ticker label |
|---|---|---|
| family | Archivo | Archivo |
| size | 11.5px | 11.5px |
| variation | `wdth 88, wght 650` | `wdth 88, wght 650` |
| case | uppercase | uppercase |
| colour (unselected) | `rgb(156,149,133)` | `rgb(156,149,133)` |
| first four strings | Air · Yamuna · Monsoon · Forest fire | Air · Yamuna DO · Monsoon · Forest fire |
| tracking | 1.495px (.13em) | 1.725px (.15em) |

Vertical separation, ink to ink: **80.3px at 1920 and 1440**, 77.9 at 1200, 74.7 at 1024,
72.5 at 900, **68.3 at 768**. Horizontal: 100px apart at 1440 and 170px at 1920 (§0.4), but
**at 1200 and below they share a left edge exactly** — 40.8/40.8, 34.8/34.8, 26.1/26.1.
So the two rows are most alike precisely where they are closest together. At 375 the gap is
130.5px with the counter and the arrows between them, and the problem does not exist.

The hero's red `412` at 272px also repeats as the ticker's red `412` at 24.5px, **274.0px
below it**, measured ink-to-ink at 1440.

**Cause.** `.rig-tabs` (home.html:454) and `.lbl` (home.html:122) both resolve to the same
micro-caps recipe, and nothing distinguishes a selector from an index.

**Cost.** One switches a panel in place; the other leaves the page. Drawn identically and
stacked, they read as one control that wrapped — a tab bar and its overflow row. The
ticker's declared status is *chrome, not a band*; today it reads as the hero's second row.
And the resemblance is not merely visual: today the ticker's first four cells **are** the
hero's four tabs in the same order, which is a coincidence of hand-authoring. Under the
client's ruling both memberships compute themselves and can disagree — at which point the
page will be showing two rows that look like one control and no longer contain the same
things. See **Q1**.

### D4 — "Delhi. Since 2000." is drawn as a column head for AIR, and it is false for two of the seven cells. *(both)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-1440-headstack.png` (5×) — "DELHI. SINCE 2000." sits directly above
"AIR", same x, same face, same size, same tracking, same colour, with only a 1.31:1 hairline
between them. `ad2-tk-375-truesize.png` shows the same stack at 1:1 on a phone, where it is
the strip's first two of three consecutive rows of micro-caps.

**Measured.** `.s-ticker-who` (home.html:1059) is `--fg-3` `rgb(156,149,133)`, 11.5px,
`.15em` tracking, uppercase. `.s-ticker-name` (home.html:1075) is `--fg-3`
`rgb(156,149,133)`, 11.5px, `.15em` tracking, uppercase. Byte-identical typography. Ink x is
46.0 and 46.0 at 1440, 216/216 at 1920, 20/20 at 375. Vertical separation 31.9px at 375.

This is the half of salvage #9 that was **not** fixed. The timestamp did get its brightness
step to `--fg-2` (10.85:1); the field that is actually stacked on a cell label did not.

**And it is factually wrong.** Two of the seven readings are not Delhi. `#h-forestloss` is
*"India / Tree cover loss — 1.65 million hectares lost, 2001 to 2025"*; `#h-fire` is
*"North India / Active fire detections"*. The strip's masthead stamps "Delhi." over both,
and "Since 2000." over a series that starts in 2001.

**Cost.** The reader's eye reads "DELHI. SINCE 2000. / AIR / 412" as a three-line cell, so
the strip appears to open with a two-line column head — and the head it appears to be is
untrue for 29% of the row. On the page whose through-line is that nothing appears without
its source attached, the strip's own source line is the one thing that misdescribes its
contents.

**Fix.** Two moves. (a) Take `.s-ticker-who` off the cell labels' exact setting: give it
`--fg-2` like its neighbour, so the whole head row is one brightness and the cell labels are
one step below it — this is the step that already exists, just applied to both fields.
(b) The geography is approved copy, so it must be cut or moved, not rewritten — see **Q3**.

### D5 — "TREATMENT 14/18" in red states the opposite of the instrument it links to. *(both)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-cage-zoom.png` (7×), cell 6.

**Measured.** The linked panel `#h-stp` reads, in its own words: *"Delhi / Sewage treatment
plants — 14 of 18 plants **failing** discharge standards"*, verdict *"Not meeting standard"*,
*"Standard is all 18. Limit broken."* The strip renders label "Treatment", value "14/18",
red. "14/18" against a label naming the thing that works reads as *fourteen of eighteen
plants working*. Red is the only signal against that reading, and three of the other six
values in the row are also counts, so red-on-a-fraction is not distinguishing.

Worse than the salvage file reported: the instrument's own copy states that the value the
backend stores is the **string "14 of 18"**, and that *"the layout has to hold that as
comfortably as it holds 412."* So "14/18" is the homepage's own abbreviation of a stored
string, not the datum.

**Cost.** The strip's job is to be glanceable. A reader who glances at exactly one cell in
this strip and takes away "most of Delhi's sewage plants are fine" has been told the
opposite of the truth by the organisation's own instrument.

**Fix.** Change the label to the instrument's own words — **"Plants failing"** — keeping the
value 14/18 in red (home.html:2008). This is moved copy, not a rewrite. **Cost to flag:**
"Plants failing" is 108.7px of ink against "Treatment"'s 79.8, which raises the width at
which seven columns still fit. At 1440 the cell content box is 115.2px, so it fits with
6.5px to spare; below ~1,360px it would ellipsise. That is acceptable only because the
strip already scrolls below 900 and, if Q2 goes to nine cells, will scroll below ~1,360
anyway. Decide Q2 first.

### D6 — The seven value rules read as the strip's bottom border, because they are 0.6px from equidistant between their numeral and the section edge. *(both)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-1440-truesize.png` at 1:1 — the bottom of the strip reads as a broken,
partly-red, partly-green horizontal line. `ad2-tk-botseam-zoom.png` (9×) shows the
arithmetic: red rule, a small gap, then the statement photograph.

**Measured at 1440×900.** Value box bottom 980.8. Rule `margin-top:.34em` = **8.3px** below
it, rule bottom 992.1. Section bottom border top edge 999.8 — **7.7px** below the rule.
The rule is 0.6px from being exactly halfway between the thing it belongs to and the thing
it does not. At 375 it is 7.1px above and **6.9px** below: a 0.2px difference. At 768,
7.1 and 8.9.

**Cost.** The rule's whole meaning is that it belongs to *its own numeral* — that is the
site's signature mark, and it is why seven rules of seven different lengths is texture
rather than mess. At 8.3 vs 7.7 the eye has no basis for assigning it, and it defaults to
the nearer structural line, which is the strip's edge. The result at working size is that
the ticker appears to have a dashed multicoloured bottom border, and the rail — the one
thing carrying the page's signature into this band — is read as a table rule.

**Fix.** Break the tie by a factor, not by a pixel. Take the cell's `padding-bottom` from
19px to 26px at ≥561 and from 17px to 22px below it (home.html:1069, 1106), so the rule sits
~8px under its numeral and ~15px above the section edge — a 1:2 ratio, which is enough to
assign it unambiguously. Cost: 7px at desktop (112.8 → ~119.8, still inside 96–120) and 5px
at 375, paid for several times over by D10's head cut.

**Where.** `ad2-tk-redgreen-zoom.png` (6×) and `ad2-tk-cage-zoom.png` — cell 7 is

Whatever is chosen, it must not use mustard and must not be a second hue.

### D8 — The cage is asymmetric and its bottom bar does not exist. *(both)*

**Where.** `ad2-tk-topseam-zoom.png` and `ad2-tk-botseam-zoom.png`, both at 9×.

**Measured, as composited against `#151512`.**

| element | value | contrast |
|---|---|---|
| section top/bottom border, `--hair` | `rgb(67,66,62)` | **1.82:1** |
| head bottom border and the six cell dividers, `--hair-2` | `rgb(44,44,40)` | **1.31:1** |
| the ground step itself, `#0D0D0B` → `#151512` | — | **1.06:1** |

Three consequences. (a) The hex change contributes essentially nothing — 1.06:1 is not a
visible step, so the "its own hex" leg of the cage is decorative and the seam is carried
entirely by one 1.82:1 hairline. (b) The bottom hairline is **not visible at all**: at 9×
(`ad2-tk-botseam-zoom.png`) there is no discernible line where the section ends; what the
reader sees is the statement band's halftone starting. The cage is open at the bottom. (c)
The six dividers that actually separate red from green are the *weakest* line in the
component, at half the weight of the outer bars — the reverse of the hierarchy a cage needs.

At 1:1 on a good desktop panel the dividers do survive (`ad2-tk-1440-truesize.png`), because
81px of continuous vertical line is legible below the contrast the same colour would need as
a dot. They do not survive a phone in daylight, and 1.31:1 is well under the 3:1 minimum for
a meaningful non-text graphic.

**Cost.** The direction is explicit that the red/green exemption is *conditional*: "To earn
that exemption it must be visually caged: its own hex, a hairline top and bottom, micro-scale
type throughout, and never any mustard." Two of the four are delivered outright (micro type,
no mustard). The hex does nothing and the bottom hairline does not render. The strip is

least as strong as the outer one, and give the section's bottom border a weight that
survives a photograph landing on it — either `--hair` at 2px or `--fg-3` at 1px. Do not
solve it by lightening the ground: `#151512` is fixed by `groundRhythm` and the ground is
not what carries this.

## 3. Questions for the client

### Q1 — The hero's deck tabs and the ticker's labels. *(AD-01's Q5, restated)*

**AD-01's recommendation was (b): keep them independent, drop the ticker labels to 10.5px
and separate the two rows by more than 90px. I agree with the ruling and overrule the
remedy.**

Agreeing with independence: making the ticker drive the hero deck gives one strip two jobs
and directly contradicts its declared status as chrome. It also breaks under the client's
own ruling — if both memberships compute seasonally, the ticker's first four cells will
stop being the hero's four tabs, and a control whose contents silently stop matching what it
controls is worse than two things that look alike. Independence, confirmed.

Overruling the remedy, on the measurements in D3:

- **10.5px will not be seen.** A 1px step on 11.5px type is under 9%, at a size where the eye
  is reading shape not scale, and the two rows still agree on family, weight, width axis,
  case and colour. It changes the spec sheet, not the page.
- **"More than 90px" is already the situation and it is not enough.** The gap is 80.3px at
  1440 — never the 90–93px both earlier documents assumed — and at 768 it is **68.3px with
  the two rows sharing a left edge**. Buying separation means adding height to the hero or to
  the strip, and both are capped.
- Widening the ticker's tracking, the other half of the salvage's suggestion, makes it look
  *more* like a tab bar, not less.

**The differentiator has to be shape, not type.** The mobile doctrine's own principle —
"cutting copy is not the strategy, changing shape is" — applies here at every width. Options:

- **(a) Flip the cell: value on top, label beneath, with the flat rail between them.**
  Then the strip's first line is seven *numbers*, not seven words, and the resemblance is
  gone at a glance rather than on inspection. It also matches the hero's own rail geometry
  (numeral first, unit and verdict subordinate to it), fixes D4's column-head stack for free
  (nothing sits under "Delhi. Since 2000." but a numeral), and moves the value rules into the
  middle of the strip where D6's ambiguity disappears. Cost: one structural change to the
  cell, and the labels lose their scanning row.
- **(b) AD-01's remedy as written** — 10.5px labels, wider tracking, more separation.
  Cheapest; for the reasons above I do not think it works.
- **(c) Make the ticker the hero deck's index** — rejected above.

**I would take (a).** It is the only option that survives the 768 case, it is a shape change
rather than a type change, and it pays for three other defects on the way past. It is the
larger build, and it changes a read the client has already seen, which is why it is a
question and not a ruling.

### Q2 — Under the validity-window ruling, what does the ticker show?

The client's ruling makes ticker membership and hero-deck membership compute themselves and
allows them to disagree. Today: nine situations defined, `intelligence.html` says six are in
window ("Heat closed on 15 July"), the ticker shows seven, the hero shows four (§0.5).

- **(a) In-window only.** The strip's length changes with the season — seven cells in August,
  perhaps five in April. `repeat(7,1fr)` becomes `repeat(n,1fr)`, the pitch changes month to
  month, and a reader returning in spring finds readings simply gone.
- **(b) All nine, always, fixed order, out-of-window cells dormant.**
- **(c) All nine, always, all live-looking** — shows a stale number as though it were current.
  Rejected outright: that is the one thing this page exists not to do.

**I would take (b), and the argument is textual rather than sloganeering.** The exemption
from the red/green rule is granted in `groundRhythm` **because "it summarises every situation
at once."** That is the stated ground for the licence. Under (a) the strip stops summarising
every situation, and the licence has no basis — the ticker would be an ordinary band with red
next to green in it. Completeness is the price of the exemption.

The second argument is that the interior pages already meet this standard and the homepage
does not. `intelligence.html` says "Heat closed on 15 July", labels the noise feed
"Not yet wired / No observation / Demo data", and its order tracker carries "Nothing filed —
that is a finding, not a gap, and it stays on the page." The homepage's only instrument strip
silently drops two of nine. The through-line is not being invoked in the abstract; the
homepage is failing a standard the rest of the site already keeps.

**What a dormant cell looks like — decided, using vocabulary this file already has.**
Dashed already means *a window that is shut*, twice over (`.closed .rl::after`
home.html:276, `.state.closed i` home.html:287), and AD-01's D9 has already been implemented
to reserve it (`.tag-season{border-style:solid}`, home.html:602, with the comment at 596
saying exactly this). So:

- label unchanged, `--fg-3`
- the value slot carries the word **CLOSED**, at the value's size but set on the *label's*
  axes (`wdth 88, wght 650`, not the numerals' 800) so it reads as chrome, not as a reading.
  74.3px of ink at 1440, 85.4 at 375 — fits every cell at every width.
- the flat rail beneath it becomes **1px dashed `--fg-3`**
- no hue, no number, no stale value. The cell stays a link.
- Explicitly **not** `.win.closed`, which paints closed windows mustard (home.html:318).
  Mustard is banned in this strip.

**The cost, which the client must accept with the answer.** Nine columns need ~115px of cell
content for the longest label. At 1440 that works (149.8px columns, 115.2px content, "Out of
river" is 93px). Below **~1,180px** it does not, and with D5's "Plants failing" (108.7px)
the floor rises to **~1,360px**. So (b) turns the strip into a horizontal scroller on most
laptops, not just phones. That is tolerable *only if D1 is fixed first* — the count in the
head is what makes a scrolled strip honest at any width. Fixing D1 is a precondition for
this answer, not a consequence of it.

### Q3 — The masthead: "Delhi. Since 2000."

It is approved copy, it is false for two of the seven cells, and at 375 it is what pushes the
head to two lines (D4, D10). Approved copy may be moved or cut, never rewritten. Three cuts,
all measured against 335px of available width at 375:

- **(a) Cut "Delhi."** → `Since 2000.` (80.6px) + gap + full timestamp (192.9px) = 293.5px,
  one line at 375. Removes the false claim outright. The strip loses its place-stamp, which
  the hero already carries ("Delhi-NCR", "CPCB continuous monitor, Anand Vihar").
- **(b) Keep "Delhi.", cut the weekday** → `Delhi. Since 2000.` + `07:00 IST today` = 272.7px,
  also one line. Cheaper on copy, but leaves "Delhi." standing over two readings that are
  North India and India.
- **(c) Keep both, and drop Forest fire and Forest loss from the strip** so "Delhi." becomes
  true. This interacts with Q2 — it is the opposite direction from nine cells, and I do not
  think the client wants a Delhi-only instrument.

**I would take (a).** It is the only one of the three that makes the head accurate rather
than merely shorter, and it is a pure cut. If the client wants the place kept, (b) is
acceptable provided the two non-Delhi cells acquire their scope somewhere — but that is new
copy and I would rather not spend it here.

### Q4 — Does the green cell get an authorship mark, and what?

The instrument page marks it twice in its own words ("Swechha field record", "Swechha's own
record, not a public feed"). The strip marks it not at all (D7). The hue exemption is closed
and is not what this question is about.

- **(a) Mark it in place.** A micro overline above the cell label — the instrument's own
  words, moved — plus a heavier divider before cell 7 so the change of authorship coincides
  with a change in the row's rhythm rather than sitting inside it. Costs ~14px of strip
  height for the overline, or nothing if it replaces nothing and the label absorbs it.
- **(b) Leave it.** The strip is an index; provenance lives one click away, and every cell's
  source is on its own instrument page. Cheapest, and defensible on the grounds that a
  113px strip cannot carry seven provenance lines.
- **(c) Take green out of the strip entirely** and let it live only in the impact band, which
  is where `groundRhythm` puts the green. Then the ticker is six public readings and the
  organisation's own number is not mixed in at all.

**I would take (a), with the divider and without the overline if height is tight.** (b) is
the status quo and it is the one place on this page where the organisation's own claim wears
someone else's authority — that is precisely the failure the through-line was written
against. (c) is tempting and I would resist it: removing the one recovered quantity from the
instrument makes the strip a list of things that are broken, which is not what this
organisation is.

## 4. What I would NOT change

**The flat rail.** `.s-ticker-v::after` at `left:0;right:0` on a `width:max-content` box.
Do not make the seven rules equal length, do not run them to the cell edge, do not give them
a minimum width. Their raggedness is the numerals speaking, and it is the hero's contract
correctly re-shaped for a 25px numeral. D6 moves the rule *away from the section edge*; it
does not touch the mechanism.

**The 3px breach against the 1px green.** Only a breach swells — that is the rail contract
verbatim, and it is why a swell means something. Do not "balance" green up to 3px, and do not
introduce a third weight.

**No mustard, anywhere, including hover and focus.** Salvage defect #7 prescribes exactly the
opposite and is now forbidden. `.s-ticker-cell:focus-visible` deliberately uses `--fg` ink
instead of the global mustard outline (home.html:1073–1074) and that is a good, deliberate
override. Leave it.

**The near-invisible hover.** A 5.5% wash plus the label stepping to `--fg`. Seven arrows,
seven underlines or seven chevrons in a 113px strip would be noise, and the cells are `<a>`
elements so the cursor already says "link". At rest the strip should look like an instrument
face, not like a menu. Resist the instinct that follows from D3.

**The 100px spine offset from the hero.** §0.4. The ticker is on `.wide` because the
direction puts it there. Do not pull it in to match the hero.

**The height, and T4.** 113px at 1440, 120px at 375. Do not apply `clamp(28px,3vw,44px)` —
see §0.3 — and do not let D6's 7px or D10's count grow the strip past 120.

**The value size.** 24.48px at 1440, 21px at 375. It is an eleventh of the hero readout and a
third of what the impact numerals will be once they go to `--t-num` (73.6px at 1440). That subordination is the whole reason a strip
carrying seven numbers does not compete with the band above it. If the strip feels weak,
that is D8's cage, not the type.

**The equal seven-column grid.** Do not go back to content-sized columns. If Q2 goes to nine,
keep them equal too; a dormant cell is the same width as a live one, because the point is
that the instrument has a fixed shape and only its readings change.

**No controls in the strip, ever.** The doctrine's rule about putting a scroller's controls
in the bottom 180px applies to scrollers that have controls. This one should not acquire
tabs, arrows or a scrollbar — D1's fix is a count in the masthead, which is a readout, not a
control. A control in the ticker would make it the tab bar it is already mistaken for.

**The `flex-wrap` masthead.** It is why nothing in this strip can be clipped mid-word again.
D10 shortens what it holds; it must not replace the wrap with a breakpoint.

**The four halftoned hero photographs above it and the statement photograph below it.**
Out of scope, and the ticker's job is to be the flat, pictureless rule between them. It is,
and that is worth more than any of the twelve defects above.
